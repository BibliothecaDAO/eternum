# Paladin pathway

Use a Paladin-owned asset brief with separate rider/mount bindings and the latest approved source. The pilot contains
useful diagnostics, not mandatory anatomy or an approved final gait. Preserve the accepted concept's cap, armor, mane,
palette and anatomy; do not reapply old repair offsets or scaling percentages to a new correct source.

The rider skin includes body/clothing/armor. Paladin weapon and forearm shield are removable same-family items. Mount,
tack and A/B/C authored coat maps have their own provenance. Calibrate natural seat, thighs, stirrups/soles and
mouth-bit reins. Establish comfortable arm/shield position before routing reins to the hand; forearm shield and rein
hand are distinct contacts. Rigid gear keeps authored dimensions. Inspect wrists, blade/handle alignment and leg/horse
clearance.

Validate bind/neutral first, then full useful front/hind leg chains, hoof soles/orientation, neck continuity and rider
contacts. Ground samples need a consistent world/rig frame. A point target on the ground does not prove the whole hoof
is correctly oriented or reachable. Never repair wrong runtime posing by stretching the original rider or concealing a
clamped unreachable limb. Fit counts 1,2,3 at common rider scale, with stable coats A, A/B, A/B/C and swept gear/tail
bounds.

Paladin controller composes the existing mount and rider lifecycles. Horse controller owns support and locomotion phase;
rider responds with restrained upper-body lag while seat/stirrup/rein contacts remain coherent. Idle, walk, run (horse
gallop), attack and defend each need independent evidence. A different mount anatomy may use a different mount adapter,
not forced horse bones. New skins normally reuse controllers with reviewed calibration. Follow
[motion review](../procedural-animation.md); keep future gait work independent of Knight/Crossbowman tuning.
