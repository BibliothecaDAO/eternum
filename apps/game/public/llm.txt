# LORDS

LORDS is the Realms token. Every game pays in it: Blitz entries, chests and prizes, and Frontier's chests.

## Test LORDS for now

Until the LORDS launch, every game runs on a test LORDS token with no value. Entries, chests, prizes and Frontier
withdrawals all pay in it. The pages here say LORDS for both; they change at the launch.

You can mint test tokens for free. Under a Blitz entry, **Mint 1,000 test tokens** mints them to your payout wallet. It
takes one signature, and the wallet pays the network fee.

<Shot name="account/mint" caption="A Blitz entry short of LORDS, with Mint 1,000 test tokens under it." />

## In Blitz

* **The entry** is paid in LORDS from your payout wallet, and a sword or a shield costs one entry each
  ([Entry and the lobby](/blitz/entry)).
* **Chests.** Every player of a finished game gets a chest that pays LORDS or an entry credit
  ([Points, results and prizes](/blitz/scoring)).
* **The season prize** goes to the season's best players by rating.

## In Frontier

* **Chests** pay LORDS into your realm ([Chests and LORDS](/frontier/chests)).
* **Refill** spends your realm's LORDS on stamina ([Armies](/frontier/armies#stamina)).
* **Withdraw** sends your realm's LORDS to your payout wallet, until the season's claim window closes.

<Shot frame="panel" name="account/withdraw" caption="A Frontier realm's LORDS, withdrawn to the payout wallet." />

## Terms

Playing is covered by the game's [Terms](https://play.realms.party/terms) and
[Privacy](https://play.realms.party/privacy) pages.

# Payout wallet

Your payout wallet is the Starknet wallet linked to your Realms account. Frontier needs none to play. You need one to:

* pay a Blitz entry, and get its refund back;
* carry a Blitz rating;
* receive Blitz chests and claim the season prize;
* withdraw your Frontier realm's LORDS;
* claim Frontier labor for the Realms the wallet holds.

A game's results, chest and prize go to the wallet its seat was played with, even if you change your payout wallet
afterwards.

## Linking a wallet

Open **Settings**, **Payout wallet**. A Blitz lobby's **Link wallet** takes you there and brings you back once it is
linked.

* **I have a wallet:** Ready (formerly Argent), Braavos or Controller.
* **I need one:** Ready, in this browser with its extension, or by email with no install. On a phone, Ready by email.

Linking takes two steps: the wallet signs a message, then you type a six-digit code sent to your account's email. Realms
creates no wallet and holds no key.

<Shot frame="panel" name="account/payout-wallet" caption="Settings, Payout wallet: I have a wallet, or I need one." />

## Replacing or unlinking

**Replace** links another wallet the same way. **Unlink** takes the emailed code alone, so you can unlink a wallet you
have lost. Nothing pays out until a wallet is linked.

## Network fees

Paying an entry, minting test tokens, opening a chest and claiming a prize are transactions your wallet signs, and it
pays their network fee in STRK. With no STRK for the fee, the page offers **Swap on AVNU**.

# Sign in

You sign in to Realms with Discord or with a code sent to your email. No wallet is needed to sign in or to play: the
device you play on signs your game actions itself.

## Connect

**Connect** opens over the page you are on: from **Play free**, from **Sign in** on You, or from a Blitz lobby. When you
are signed in, you go on to where you were going, or stay on the same page.

<Shot name="account/connect" caption="Connect: Discord, or your email and Send code." />

**With Discord.** Press **Discord** and approve Realms there. If Discord finishes in another browser (the Discord app on
a phone often opens its own), that browser shows a code. Type it into Connect, under "Discord finished in another
window? Enter the code it shows."

**With email.** Type your email and press **Send code**. A six-digit code arrives by email; type it in, and you are
signed in on the sixth digit. Connect shows when the code expires, and offers **New code** once it has. Tap your address
to change it.

<Shot name="account/code" caption="The code step: the address it went to, six boxes, and when the code expires." />

## Your name and portrait

Your first sign-in creates your Realms account. Connect then asks for a name, suggested from your Discord name or your
email, and a portrait. **Claim name** saves both. You can change either later in Settings.

<Shot name="account/name" caption="A new account claims its name and picks a portrait." />

## Devices

Each browser you sign in on keeps its own key, and that key signs your game actions on that device. An account can play
on 8 devices. Entering a game on a ninth asks you to remove one: on a device that is already signed in, open
**Settings**, **Devices**, remove one, then try again.

Removing a device signs it out, and it cannot enter a game until it signs in again. **Sign out** in Settings makes this
device forget your account until you sign in again.

<Shot name="account/settings" frame="panel" caption="Settings, Account: your name, portrait, sign-in methods and payout wallet." />

# Armies and combat

An army holds one troop type at one tier. Each hex holds one army.

## Starting forces

Each of your three realms is dealt one troop type, one realm Knights, one Crossbowmen and one Paladins, in an order
drawn for you. Each starts with 1,000 T1 troops of its type garrisoned and
1,000 in reserve. No army stands on the map at the start: create one from the reserve.

Barracks make more: T1 troops from wheat and a resource, T2 from T1 troops, T3 from T2
([Realms and production](/blitz/realms#production)).

## Tiers and deployment

A troop's strength is its count times its tier's strength: 1 for T1,
3 for T2, 9 for T3. A realm's level caps the strength of one army
([Realms and production](/blitz/realms#levels)), and the largest army is that cap divided by its tier's strength,
times  0.5 for T1, 1 for T2 and
1.5 for T3.

**Field armies** stand on the map. Select your realm and right-click a free tile beside it to deploy one there, or use
the Military window (`M`). A new army starts with 30 stamina.

**Guards** stand in a structure's guard slots and do not appear on the map. An attacker meets them one slot at a time. A
slot whose guard falls cannot take a new one for 10 minutes. A guard can attack an army
beside its structure.

<Shot frame="panel" name="blitz/deploy" caption="Deploying beside a realm: how many troops, the stamina they start on, and the cost." />

## Merging and moving troops

Select an army, then right-click another of your armies or one of your structures beside it: the transfer panel moves
troops between them, across your realms too. Both sides must hold the same troop type and tier; an empty guard slot
takes any type and tier, and only from an army. A guard cannot move straight to another guard slot. The army that
receives troops keeps the lower of the two staminas and the longer of the two battle timers.

## Stamina

A T1 army holds 120 stamina, a T2 army 140 and a T3 army 160. Each
recovers 30 every 60 seconds. Exploring a tile costs 30.
A step onto a known tile costs 20, 10 more or less by biome and troop type.
Moving and exploring also cost 0.05 wheat a troop, from the army's home realm.

<BiomeStamina mode="blitz" />

## Attacking

Select an army and right-click an enemy within reach: the attack forecast opens with each side's losses, and **Attack**
sends it. Attacking takes 50 stamina; without it, the attack is refused. The defender spends
40 stamina, and deals 30% less damage without it.

<Shot frame="panel" name="blitz/attack-forecast" caption="The attack forecast: each side's troops before and after." />

**Biomes.** Each troop type fights 30% better or worse on some biomes. The biome is the defender's
tile.

<BiomeCombat mode="blitz" />

**Damage.** Both sides strike in the same exchange. Each side deals 2 × its troops × its tier's
weight ÷ the other side's tier weight ÷ (both sides' troops together)<sup>0.2</sup>. The tier
weights are 1 for T1, 3 for T2 and
9 for T3. On top of that:

* the biome adds or takes 30%, as the table above shows;
* a defender without the stamina to defend deals 30% less;
* a defender still on its battle timer deals 15% less;
* relics and the troop roles below add their own.

Blitz rolls no dice, so the attack forecast is the result. For example, 1,000 Knights attack
1,000 T1 Knights on grassland, where Knights have no biome edge:

| Attacking tier | Defenders lost | Attackers lost | Attacker's battle timer | Attacker's stamina spent |
| -------------- | -------------- | -------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------ |
| T1             | 437            | 437            | 60 seconds              | 50                       |
| T2             | 1,000          | 146            | 8 seconds               | 6                        |
| T3             | 1,000          | 49             | 0 seconds               | 0                        |

**Battle timer.** An army cannot attack while its battle timer runs. A fight at close range adds up to
60 seconds to each side's timer and spends the stamina the fight took. A side that dealt
more than 2.5 times the damage it took has less time added and gets some of that stamina back. At
10 times or more it has none added and gets all of it back. A ranged attack adds
30 seconds to both timers.

The time is added to what is left on a timer, so a defender attacked again before its timer ends waits longer. For
example, 1,000 T1 Knights attack 1,000 T1 Knights with
60 seconds left on their timer: the defenders then wait
120 seconds and the attackers
60\. If 1,000 T1 Crossbowmen attack them from range
instead, the defenders wait 90 seconds and the Crossbowmen
30\.

<Video name="blitz/battle" caption="A battle: the attacker strikes, the defender falls." />

## Troop roles

* **Knights** deal 15% more damage attacking a structure's guards from beside it, and as guards
  take 15% less damage.
* **Crossbowmen** can attack from 2 hexes away. A ranged attack still costs the attacker
  50 stamina, but the target spends only 20 and strikes no blow back. It
  deals 70% of its damage to an army and 30% to a structure's
  guards, ignores biomes, and adds half as much to the battle timers. A ranged attack never claims a structure.
* **Paladins** have no combat bonus, and open ground costs them the least stamina.

## Capturing

When a structure's last guard falls, an army beside it claims it. Realms, camps, Essence Rifts and Hyperstructures can
all be captured, and a captured realm's armies go with it. A player who loses their last realm watches the rest of the
game.

# Entry and the lobby

## When games run

A new Blitz slot starts every 90 minutes, on a fixed grid of the UTC day, while the season's Blitz
window is open. Play shows the next ranked Blitz: when it starts and when its entry opens, in your local time.

<Shot name="blitz/next" caption="Play's Blitz column: the next ranked game, its start, and when its entry opens." />

## Joining

Join a slot from Play: entry is paid in LORDS from your linked payout wallet, at the season's entry fee (100 LORDS this
season), shown on every slot. Link a payout wallet in Settings first; the slot's **Link wallet** takes you there and
back ([Payout wallet](/account/payout-wallet)). Until the LORDS launch the entry is paid in test LORDS, and **Mint 1,000
test tokens** under the entry tops your wallet up ([LORDS](/account/lords)).

<Shot name="blitz/entry" caption="A slot's entry: the seat, the sword and the shield, the total, and Pay & join." />

## Sword and shield

At entry you can add a sword, which doubles your rating gain in that game, and a shield, which halves your rating loss.
Each costs one entry. A sword or shield credit won from a chest is used before LORDS
([Points, results and prizes](/blitz/scoring#ratings)).

## Your entry

Your entry reads **Registered** once paid, then **Seated** when your seat is drawn. A paid entry is **Not seated** when
the wallet that paid was not the payout wallet of your Realms account, or when your account already holds a seat in that
slot: the earlier entry keeps it. A slot that is cancelled leaves every entry unseated.

An unseated entry is refunded in full, with any sword or shield you paid for, to the wallet that paid. Press **Take
refund** on the slot in Play once it shows.

## The lobby

Registered players write in their slot's lobby chat; any signed-in player can read it. The lobby counts down to entry
closing, then to the start. On a desktop, the roster shows each seat's Blitz rating.

<Shot name="blitz/lobby" caption="A seated player's lobby: who is in, the countdown, and the chat." />

## When entry closes

Entry closes 5 minutes before the games start. There is no cap on a slot: at the close, everyone
seated is split into games of up to 24 players, as even as can be and in the order they joined. For
example, 25 players form games of 13 and 12. The game settles three realms for each player, with their stores and their
troops. It opens at its start time once its whole roster is seated, and ends at its scheduled end. A player who is not
on the roster can watch.

# Blitz

A Blitz is a 60-minute game for up to 24 players. Each player starts with three
realms, and the most points at the end wins. Entry is paid in LORDS, every player gets a chest, and the season's best
share its prize.

<Video name="blitz/an-hour" caption="A Blitz map: realms, Hyperstructures rising, armies meeting." />

## How an hour goes

1. **Build.** Your three realms start with stores, a garrison and a reserve of one troop type each. Build farms,
   resource buildings and barracks, and level your realms up.
2. **Explore.** Armies reveal the map tile by tile. Each reveal scores points and sends Essence, labor or donkeys home.
3. **Take.** Camps, Essence Rifts and Hyperstructures are guarded by bandits. Clear the guard and claim the structure
   for points; a Hyperstructure keeps scoring for as long as you hold it.
4. **Fight.** Every realm can be attacked and captured from the first second. Defend yours and take others'.

When the 60 minutes end, the game records every player's points and place
([Points, results and prizes](/blitz/scoring)).

## What you can do

* Play three realms, each dealt one troop type: Knights, Crossbowmen or Paladins.
* Build, produce resources and troops, and let automation run production (Smart, Custom or Off).
* Send goods and troops between your own holdings by donkey.
* Deploy field armies, guard your structures, and merge armies across your realms.
* Explore for Essence, labor and donkeys, and find dormant spires.
* Attack any army or structure. Crossbowmen attack from 2 hexes away.
* Capture realms, camps, Essence Rifts and Hyperstructures.
* Open relic crates and activate their relics.
* Chat with the other players.

## What you can't do

* Trade, or send anything to another player.
* Raid a structure for its resources.
* Produce resources from labor.
* Bridge anything into or out of the game.
* Form an alliance: every other player is an enemy.
* Shelter behind immunity: no realm has any.
* Keep playing after you lose your last realm. You can watch until the end.

## Next

* [Entry and the lobby](/blitz/entry): get a seat in the next game.
* [Realms and production](/blitz/realms): grow three realms into an economy.
* [Transfers and donkeys](/blitz/transport): move goods and troops between your holdings.
* [Armies and combat](/blitz/armies): field armies and win fights.
* [The map](/blitz/map): find and take what scores.
* [Points, results and prizes](/blitz/scoring): what scores, and what a result pays.

# The map

## The layout

The map starts dark. At the start everyone sees every realm with the tiles around it, and every Hyperstructure site.
Once a tile is explored, everyone sees it.

Hyperstructure sites stand in rings around the centre of the map, 12 hexes apart, and the number of
rings grows with the number of players in the game:

<HyperstructureRings />

Each player's three realms stand close together, three hexes apart, among the rings.

## Exploring

An army beside an unexplored tile can explore it, for 30 stamina. The tile is revealed to everyone,
scores 5 points, and pays the army's home realm one of the finds below: a share of the army's strength
(its troop count times its tier's strength), counted up to 3,000. An Exploration Reward relic adds its
bonus on top.

<ExplorationRewards />

<Video name="blitz/explore" caption="An army explores, and each tile it reveals is drawn as it lands." />

A revealed tile can also hold something:

| Find            | Chance per reveal |
| --------------- | ----------------- |
| A dormant spire | 0.5%              |
| An Essence Rift | 2%                |
| A camp          | 2.9%              |

**Dormant spires.** The finder's realm receives 1,000 Essence once, plus any active Exploration
Reward relic bonus. The spire then stands on its tile for the rest of the game, blocking it.

## Relic crates

When a player explores and no crate has appeared for 5 minutes, a relic crate appears within
10 hexes of the explored tile, where everyone can see it. An army beside it opens it: its pack of
three relics is revealed, the army keeps the army relics, and the realm relics go to that army's home realm. Opening a
crate scores 250 points.

<RelicPackTable />

<Video frame="wide" name="blitz/open-crate" caption="A relic crate opened beside an army, and its three relics." />

## Relics

A relic is activated by the army or the realm that holds it, for Essence. A timed relic works for
3 minutes.

<RelicTable />

## Camps, Essence Rifts and Hyperstructures

Each is held by bandits until a player clears its guard and claims it ([Armies and combat](/blitz/armies#capturing)).

* **Camps** start with one T1 guard of 999–1,499 troops. A claimed camp holds 500 wheat,
  5,000 labor and 1,000 donkeys, makes 1 labor a
  second, runs production, and fields one army.
* **Essence Rifts** start with one T1 guard of 999–1,499 troops. A held rift makes
  10 Essence a second, 36,000 in all.
* **Hyperstructures** are founded by any player on a site: double-click the site, or press **Create here** on its panel.
  It rises guarded by three T2 bandit guards of 1,499–1,999 troops each. Claiming it scores points once, and
  holding it scores points every second ([Points, results and prizes](/blitz/scoring)).

# Realms and production

## What each realm starts with

Each of your three realms starts as a Settlement with 1,000 wheat, 1,500 labor,
360 wood, 240 coal, 120 copper and 500 donkeys. It is
dealt one troop type, and holds 1,000 T1 troops of it in its guard and 1,000 in reserve
([Armies and combat](/blitz/armies#starting-forces)).

Your realms are as likely to be attacked as anyone's: no realm has immunity, from the first second to the last.

## Levels

A realm levels up from Settlement to City, Kingdom and Empire. Each level opens another ring of plots, more field armies
and guard slots, and a bigger army:

| Level      | Plots | Field armies | Guard slots | Deployment cap |
| ---------- | ----- | ------------ | ----------- | -------------- |
| Settlement | 6     | 1            | 1           | 3,000          |
| City       | 18    | 3            | 2           | 15,000         |
| Kingdom    | 36    | 5            | 3           | 45,000         |
| Empire     | 60    | 8            | 4           | 90,000         |

Reaching City costs nothing; the next levels cost:

Level a realm up with the arrow on its row in your holdings list. Hover its **i** for what it still needs.

## Buildings

Tap an open plot, pick a tab (Resources, Economic or Military) and a building. Most buildings can be paid two ways: in
labor alone, or in less labor plus resources. The rarest resources and the T2 and T3 troop buildings take resources
only. Every building except a workers' hut takes population; each workers' hut adds capacity, on top of the realm's
base. Each further copy of a building costs more than the last.

## Production

Each realm's keep makes 2 labor a second, and each farm 6 wheat a
second, with no input. Everything else is made from other materials: a run pays all its inputs when it starts, then
makes its output over time. Camps run production too. Labor cannot be turned into resources.

<BlitzStandardResourceProduction />

<BlitzStandardTroopProduction />

<BlitzDonkeyProduction />

Start a run from **Production** (`P`): type the output you want, or what to spend of an input.

## Automation

Each realm's card offers automatic production, top right:

* **Smart**, the default: production runs itself, spread across the realm's buildings.
* **Custom**: production follows the shares you set in Production.
* **Off**: nothing is produced automatically.

Automation runs in your browser while the game is open. A realm whose only producers are farms and workers' huts has
nothing to automate: wheat and labor produce on their own.

# Points, results and prizes

## What scores

| Action                                       | Points                              |
| -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Explore a tile                               | 5                                   |
| Claim a camp or an Essence Rift from bandits | 250                                 |
| Open a relic crate                           | 250                                 |
| Claim a Hyperstructure from bandits          | 1,000                               |
| Hold a Hyperstructure                        | 1 a second for each realm around it |

A claim scores only when it is taken from bandits; capturing a structure from another player scores nothing. A held
Hyperstructure counts the player realms standing on the six realm sites around it, so it scores from nothing to six
times its rate each second, for the player who holds it.

## Standings and the end

Your name chip in the top bar shows your place and points; click it for the leaderboard, with each player's points by
kind. When the 60 minutes end, the game shows **Game finished**, and **See the results** opens your
place, the board and the chest your result earned.

<Shot name="blitz/results" caption="A finished game's results: your place and points, and your chest." />

## Where an entry goes

Every entry, and every sword and shield bought, is split two ways: 90% to the chest reserve and 10% to the treasury.
Games pay no prizes of their own; what the chests leave at the season's end pays the season's best.

## Results

At the end, the game settles its hyperstructure points and records every player's final victory points and rank. Equal
scores share a rank: scores of 100, 100 and 50 rank 1, 1 and 3. Players with zero points share last place, including
registered players who never acted.

Results, ratings, chests and season prizes all go to the wallet your seat was played with, even if you change your
payout wallet afterwards.

## A chest for every player

Every player of a settled game gets a chest, sealed until opened. Opening it draws one reward: one entry's worth of
LORDS (99.98%), a sword credit (0.01%) or a shield credit (0.01%). A credit pays for that extra at your next entry. When
the draw is LORDS but the chest reserve cannot pay the whole amount, the chest draws again between the two credits.
Opening takes two steps: **Open**, then the reveal a few blocks later. At the season's end, what is left of the chest
reserve joins the prize pool.

<Shot name="blitz/opened-chest" caption="An opened chest on the results page: what it paid, sent to the wallet that opened it." />

## Ratings

Every Blitz game is rated, however many play; only a game of one leaves ratings as they were. Ratings are measured
against a reference mean of 1,500. A game's base rating change is capped at 45, less in a small game; then a sword
doubles a gain and a shield halves a loss, in the game you bought them for, so a sword's gain can reach twice the cap.
In a rated game, the leaderboard shows each player's rating change if the game ended now, worked out as the result will
be.

## The season prize

When the season ends, ratings freeze. The best 20% of the season's players by rating share the prize pool, the top place
the largest share and each place after it 96% of the share above. The Season page shows, while the season runs, what
each place would take if it ended now.

<Shot name="blitz/season" caption="The Blitz rating board, with the prize each paid place would take now." />

Once the season's list is posted and its one-hour challenge window has passed, a winner presses **Claim** on the Season
page, from the wallet their seats were played with.

# Transfers and donkeys

You can send goods and troops between your own holdings: your realms, camps, Essence Rifts and Hyperstructures. You
cannot send anything to another player.

## Sending

Open **Transfer** (`T`) from the holding you send from, or right-click another of your holdings on the map. Pick where
it goes. With several destinations, each good's total splits evenly among them; type a destination's share to fix it.
Each destination and **Send** say when the goods land.

<Shot name="blitz/transfer" caption="Transfer from one realm to two holdings, with when each shipment lands." />

## Donkeys

Every shipment is carried by donkeys. One donkey carries 50 kg, and the donkeys a shipment needs
are used up by it. Donkeys come from a market ([Realms and production](/blitz/realms#production)).

<BlitzWeightTable />

## Arriving

A shipment travels by distance, and troops travel slower than goods. It lands at the end of the
3-minute delivery tick its trip ends in, in the destination's **Arrivals**, a tab of the
Transfer window. A shipment cannot be called back once it has left.

**Repeat** on a delivered shipment adds it to the realm's automation.

# React Client

The game client uses React for its interface and three.js for its map. The shared client in `packages/core` owns
synchronization and gameplay access; browser setup is in `apps/game/src/services/game-client.ts`.

## Run locally

Build the workspace packages, then point the client at an isolated native deployment:

```sh
pnpm install --frozen-lockfile
pnpm run build:packages
pnpm --dir apps/game dev
```

The app reads our directory at `/api/directory` (proxied to staging in development) and opens every shard it lists; a
shard's URL is its Herald. The client reads the chain id, node and admission endpoints, contracts and release from the
shard's `/manifest`, and refuses a shard whose schema differs from the client's generated bindings. Players open a shard
the directory does not list, such as a local one, by pasting its URL into the games list. See `deploy/athanor/README.md`
for the complete isolated stack setup. Do not put deployment private keys in the browser environment.

## Read current facts

`useGame` (`apps/game/src/hooks/context/game-context.tsx`) exposes the active `setup.store` and account.
`useNativeRevision` subscribes a component to the models it renders. Read rows by their native keys; game-scoped keys
begin with `game_id`.

The generated `NativeFactStore` holds typed rows with bigints and enums. It provides maps per model, game and owner
indexes, and a change feed. Missing required rows are synchronization errors, not zero-valued game facts.

Herald sends a confirmed snapshot followed by ordered diffs and a shared pre-confirmed overlay. Each transaction applies
atomically. The sync runtime feeds the store; the spatial projection derives the indexes used by map managers. React
components and scenes must not maintain another authoritative copy.

## Submit actions

Player actions use the shared client's typed command path. Commands are encoded from the compiled contract ABI, signed
with the registered gameplay key and submitted to admission. Each accepted action receives recorded context and a root.
A transaction can contain several actions, so outcomes are matched by ticket identity.

The UI may show local pending feedback. It must not predict, override or directly fetch persistent game rows. Shared
provisional state comes from Herald; story events and local effects are transient.

## Extend the client

Declare new facts in the native schema and regenerate the bindings. Add the contract command and its rule tests before
wiring its typed client caller. Use the existing store and sync seams rather than a new query service or state cache.

```sh
pnpm --dir apps/game test
pnpm --dir apps/game typecheck
pnpm run format
pnpm run knip
```

# Native contracts

The game lives in `contracts/l3/world-native`. A small set of domain contracts owns storage and rules for persistent
worlds. Games are keyed by `game_id`; creating a game does not deploy another world. Presets are immutable for a game.

## Facts and storage

The fact declarations in `schema/fact-models.mjs` drive generated bindings for Herald and the client store. Give each
fact one owner and include `game_id` first in game-scoped keys. Avoid redundant rows and derived values stored twice.
Configuration tables belong in immutable deployment/game configuration rather than generated rule code.

Persistent mutations emit the native row events. The codec identifies the model, keys and serialized values; Herald
validates these events and folds all changes from one transaction atomically. Ephemeral stories use their event codec
and recover through history. They are not persistent entity rows.

## Commands and authentication

The season domain receives recorded commands. It checks authority and actor authentication before nonce consumption,
verifies the canonical command commitment and dispatches to the owning domain. Internal commands authenticate their
calling peer. Do not expose generic row setters or an arbitrary actor override.

Recorded context governs gameplay time and randomness. Domain code consumes the root assigned to the accepted action; it
does not sample another root on a retry. The node owns ordering and its volatile admission lifecycle, while recorded
effects are protected against duplicate execution.

## Tests and generated artifacts

Use the versions in `contracts/l3/world-native/.tool-versions` for native contracts and the protocol package. The native
gate runs once on the final tree, from the repository root:

```sh
bash contracts/l3/check-native.sh
```

It builds the contracts, checks class sizes and ABI/schema/format drift, and runs the native suite with its pinned
Foundry toolchain.

When a declaration changes, run `scarb run schema` and review the generated ABI, row and command changes together with
their callers. Test expected gameplay values and rejections directly. Every class must stay below 75,366 CASM felts for
headroom under the chain's 81,920-felt limit. Shrink configuration/code duplication first; split by fact ownership only
when necessary.

# Getting Started

Eternum runs native Cairo domains on Madara. Herald folds chain events and streams snapshots and transaction diffs to
the client's native fact store. React and three.js render from that store.

## Workspace setup

Install Node.js, the pnpm version in the root `packageManager` field, and Bun. Native contract tools are pinned in
`contracts/l3/world-native/.tool-versions`; use those versions for Scarb, Starknet Foundry and the Sierra compiler.

```sh
pnpm install --frozen-lockfile
pnpm run build:packages
```

## Isolated development stack

Follow `deploy/athanor/README.md` to configure a separate Madara node, PostgreSQL database, native deployment, Herald,
identity service and admission endpoint. Ports, volumes and credentials belong to that environment. Do not run a
development deployment against a live game stack.

Native deployment and launch use the existing CLI under `config/deployer/clean/cli`. Supply a native manifest path and
required environment credentials. Regenerate each mode's balance configuration (`config/generated/`) from its source
with:

```sh
pnpm run config:generate
```

The [client guide](/development/client) explains local startup and the state/command path. Deployment credentials never
belong in a browser build.

## Checks

```sh
pnpm --dir apps/game test
pnpm --dir packages/core exec vitest run
pnpm --dir apps/herald test
pnpm run format
pnpm run knip
```

For native contract changes, run the combined gate once on the final tree:

```sh
bash contracts/l3/check-native.sh
```

It checks the build, class sizes, ABI, schema and format drift, and the native test suite. The
[contract guide](/development/contracts) covers generated artifacts and domain rules.

## Project structure

* `contracts/l3/world-native`: native game domains, schema and rule tests.
* `contracts/l3/randomness-protocol`: recorded execution types and conformance tests.
* `apps/herald`: chain fold, snapshots, diffs and history.
* `apps/game`: React and three.js client.
* `apps/launch-service`: native game launch orchestration.
* `packages/core`: shared client, fact store, sync runtime and gameplay managers.
* `packages/provider`, `packages/types`: command encoding and shared types. React hooks live in `apps/game`.
* `config`: balance configuration and native deployment tools.

Build documentation locally with `pnpm build:docs` or run `pnpm dev:docs` while editing. The build also writes the whole
site as one text file, `llm.txt`, which the game serves at `https://play.realms.party/llm.txt`.

# Game Entry

<SeasonOneArchive />

Eternum takes place in 'Seasons', multi-week campaigns with long-term strategic and diplomatic developments. There are
several weeks between each season that allow for iterative development and feature expansion in each subsequent season.

## Realms NFT Holder Entry <img src="/images/ring_pass.png" alt="Season entry" width="300" className=" float-right" />

Realms NFT holders have the exclusive benefit of free entry into each season of Eternum through the minting of Season
Passes. Each Season Pass is unique to the Realm it was minted from and allows the holder to initiate a settling
transaction to enter the game. To do this, Lords first minted the Season Pass with the wallet that held the Realms NFT,
then transferred it to their Cartridge wallet. (The Season 1 mint site is no longer online.)

## Non-Realm Holder Entry

⚖️ **Season Pass Marketplace**

Should a Realm NFT holder decide not to use their Season Pass, they could transfer it or sell it on the Season Pass
Marketplace (no longer online), providing an avenue for non-Realm holders to acquire Season Passes to participate in the
game.

🏕️ **Villages**

Villages are modest outposts that can be established around settled Realms, offering an accessible entry point into
Eternum. Villages have some restrictions but offer a low-cost, casual taste of Eternum gameplay to newcomers while also
acting as an avenue for established players to expand their production. Each Realm can support up to six surrounding
Villages.

For detailed information about Villages, including settling mechanics, resource production, and progression paths, visit
our [Villages guide](/eternum/realm-and-villages/villages).

## Realm Control Mechanics

As long as you maintain control of a settled Realm or Village, you can actively participate without additional entry
fees, engage in the market using **$LORDS** tokens, and enjoy uninterrupted gameplay until the end of the season.
However, Eternum is a high-stakes game, it can be unforgiving and players must accept that their valuable holdings may
become a target for other players. Thankfully, the game offers high-stakes and low-stakes gameplay to ensure that
players can participate in the world at their own pace and risk tolerance.

⚔️ **Realms - High Stakes**

Once settled, Realms can be conquered and claimed within a Season, transferring control to the conqueror. Realms are the
key playing pieces in Eternum and allow for the full gameplay experience in producing resources, training armies,
conducting trade, and pursuing territorial expansion. This level of capability comes with increased risk, as Realms must
continually manage defenses, alliances, and strategic positioning to protect themselves from rivals.

🌴 **Villages - Low Stakes**

Unlike Realms, Villages cannot be conquered by other players, making them an ideal choice for casual players seeking a
lower-risk gameplay experience. Villages can still participate in most gameplay activities, but production rates are
limited to 50% the rate of Realms and progression paths are limited. These features make Villages perfect for new
players looking to learn the game mechanics without the constant threat of conquest.

# Key Concepts

<SeasonOneArchive />

<img src="/images/covers/07.png" alt="Eternum Key Concepts" className="w-full rounded-lg my-6" />

This section outlines the core mechanics and systems that form the foundation of Eternum's gameplay.

## Realms

* **Limited Assets**: 8,000 unique and irreplaceable Realms serve as the foundation of Eternum, each has the ability to
  freely mint a Season Pass.
* **Settling**: Players must burn a Season Pass to settle their Realm in the game world.
* **Starting Materials**: Each newly settled Realm begins with a set of starting materials.
* **Progression Path**: Realms can upgrade from Settlements → Cities → Kingdoms → Empires, unlocking additional
  buildable hexes and defensive army slots.
* **Resource Production**: Each Realm can produce 1-7 different resources based on the original Realms NFT metadata.
* **Wonders**: Realms with Wonders have a 20% production bonus and share this bonus with all Realms and Villages in a
  12- tile radius.
* **Hardcore Gameplay**: If a Realm’s defenses crumble, it can be claimed by another player—transferring gameplay rights
  to the conqueror.

## Villages

* **Accessible Entry**: Villages can be established around settled Realms, offering a simple entry system for new
  players
* **Scaling**: Up to 6 Villages per Realm, allowing for 48,000 Villages in Season 1
* **Limited Production**: Produce materials at 50% the rate of Realms and only have access to 1 resource type each
* **Parent Realm**: Each Village is tied to a parent Realm and will pay bridging fees if moving materials out of the
  game
* **Troop Restrictions**: Villages may only receive tokenized troops from their parent Realm, they cannot buy or be sent
  troops from any other source
* **Simple Progression**: Villages have a limited upgrade path from Settlement to City
* **Casual Gameplay**: Villages can be raided, but can’t be claimed by other players.

## Materials

* **Materials Categories**:
  * **Food**: (Wheat, Fish) - essential for production, troop movement, and construction.
  * **Resources**: 22 different resource types, distributed based on rarity across both Realms and Villages.
  * **Troops**: Units with unique properties that can be assigned to armies.
  * **Donkeys**: Required for moving materials around the world, essential for logistics.
  * **Labor**: A new material in S1 that allows players to build and produce in 'simple mode', acquired through burning
    resources.
  * **Ancient Fragments**: Found in-game, critical for the construction of Hyperstructures.
* **ERC20 Compatibility**: All materials (except Labor) can be traded freely and bridged out as ERC20 tokens through
  Realms.

## Buildings

* **Construction Modes**: Two building systems available - Standard (resource-efficient) and Simple (labor-focused).
* **Building Types**:
  * **The Keep**: Central structure automatically constructed when settling, provides baseline population capacity.
  * **Resource Buildings**: Specific buildings for each of the 22 resources, only requiring food to construct.
  * **Economic Buildings**: Includes Farms, Fishing Villages, Worker Huts and Markets.
  * **Military Buildings**: Nine different buildings for producing three tiers of each troop type.
* **Placement & Population**: Buildings require buildable hexes and population capacity, with more unlocked as Realms
  and Villages are upgraded.

## Production

* **Production Modes**: Like buildings, production can be done in Standard (resource-intensive) or Simple (food and
  labor) mode.
* **Production Types**:
  * **Food**: The only resources (Wheat, Fish) that can be produced without inputs.
  * **Resources**: Require combinations of other resources (standard) or labor (simple).
  * **Labor**: Produced by burning resources in the Keep, used for simple mode construction and production.
  * **Troops**: Essential for exploration and warfare, with higher tiers requiring lower tier troops as inputs.
  * **Donkeys**: One-time-use transport, consumed after a single journey.

## World Map

* **Exploration System**: The world begins shrouded in mystery, with only the six Banks visible at season start;
  adjacent hexes are revealed when settling, and armies can explore to reveal new areas.
* **Biomes**: 16 unique biome types procedurally generated across the map, each affecting combat effectiveness and troop
  movement.
* **Movement & Stamina**: Armies move using stamina (regenerating at +20 per Phase,
  +120 per Eternum Day), with costs varying by biome and troop type; Knights, Crossbowmen, and
  Paladins each have different maximum stamina capacities.
* **Exploration Mechanics**: Exploring costs 30 stamina per hex and reveals that area
  permanently to all Lords; exploring armies may discover resources or encounter world structures and agent armies.

## Military

<img src="/images/covers/04.png" alt="Eternum Military" className="w-full rounded-lg my-6" />

* **Army Composition**: Armies consist of a single troop type and tier (Knights, Crossbowmen, or Paladins).
* **Troop Tiers**: T1, T2, and T3 troops with increasing combat strength.
* **Combat Factors**: Battles resolved based on army damage, stamina modifiers, and biome effects.
* **Army Types**:
  * **Field Armies**: Deploy onto the world map for exploration and conquest.
  * **Guard Armies**: Deployed to defense slots to protect structures.
* **Stamina System**: Armies recover +20 stamina per Phase (6 phases per Eternum Day,
  totaling +120 stamina per day).
* **Biome Effects**: Each troop type has advantages (+30% damage), disadvantages (-30% damage), or no change (0%) in
  specific biomes.
* **Raiding Mechanics**: Armies can attempt to steal resources from structures without defeating defenders.
  * Success depends on the raiding army's damage relative to the combined guard armies.
  * Undefended structures can be raided without casualties.

## Tribes

Tribes are player-formed organizations enabling cooperation and coordination. They can be made public (open to all) or
private (invitation only) and allow players access to tribe-only features. Tribes are an early form of social structures
within Eternum and rely on player-driven interactions including arrangements of alliances and declarations of war.

## Victory Conditions

Eternum's seasonal gameplay culminates in victory through the accumulation of victory points earned through the
construction and ownership of Hyperstructures. However, there are various achievement paths, with players earning
recognition and rewards for various actions within the Season.

## Victory Points

Ultimate victory in Eternum is measured by Victory Points (VP), earned exclusively through Hyperstructures. When a
single player’s total VP reaches the season-defined threshold, they unlock the ability to End Season, immediately
halting all further gameplay actions and cementing the final standings. Thus, the race to build, seize, and defend
Hyperstructures is the decisive path that ultimately closes the chapter on each season of Eternum.

## Activating Foundations

Each Hyperstructure is unique, requiring varying quantities of all 22 resources (and Labor) for completion. To activate
a Hyperstructure Foundation, all **20,000,000 Ancient
Fragments** must be deposited into the structures's balance, and then the owner of the mine must explicitly activate the
foundation to begin construction. Upon activation, the exact quantity required for each resource is randomly determined
within predefined minimum and maximum ranges shown below:

<HyperstructureInitializationShardsTable />

<HyperstructureConstructionCostTable />

*Note: In addition to these resources, all Hyperstructures require a fixed contribution of 50,000,000 units of labor to
complete construction.*

## Construction Victory Points

The Lord initiating the Hyperstructure construction determines whether contributions can be made by all Lords (public)
or restricted to members of their tribe (private). Contributions may be made directly from Realms or Villages without
requiring donkeys for transport. As Lords contribute to the Hyperstructure, they are awarded with a proportional
allocation of Victory Points. A total of **499,836** are
available to claim throughout the construction process.

## Accumulation Victory Points

Separately, and in addition to the victory points awarded at completion, the owner of the Hyperstructure begins to
accumulate victory points, **1** for every second the
structure remains under their control. These accumulating victory points can be shared among multiple Lords through a
customizable share split, allowing the owner to distribute glory and reward cooperation. If a Hyperstructure is claimed
by another Lord, future accumulated victory points are redirected to the new owner, who may similarly decide on sharing
arrangements.

## Total Victory

Victory points earned through construction contributions or accumulation cannot be lost once awarded. The first player
to reach **10,000,000 total victory points** unlocks the ability to select the ‘End
Season’ option, concluding the current game season.

# World Physics

<SeasonOneArchive />

<img src="/images/covers/08.png" alt="Troops Moving Across Eternum" className="w-full rounded-lg my-6" />

In Eternum, every action is governed by onchain physics—immutable rules encoded directly onto the blockchain, ensuring
fairness, transparency, and absolute consistency for all Lords. Here, code is law; no exceptions exist outside the
boundaries set by these protocols.

## Seasons

Welcome to Season 1. Eternum, in its current form, comes and goes in seasonal launches. Each season marks a special time
of iteration and advancement in this new form of onchain gaming and may be likened to the movement of tides. Once a
season launches, the tide flows out and the Lords arrive to build their castles in the sand. Nothing, not even the
developers, can stop or alter the course of a season once launched, and players are free to take any action that the
rules of the game allow. Once a Lord accumulates the requisite Victory Points, they unlock the ability to end the
season—signalling the tide to come back in and put an end to the Lords' fun… until next season.

## Time

Time within a season is measured in Eternum Days, and each is precisely one hour in length. Each Eternum Day is divided
into six equal phases (early hours, dawn, morning, afternoon, dusk, and late evening), with each phase lasting ten
minutes. One day ends and the next begins at the top of every real-world hour.

## Materials

In the world of Eternum, everything holds tangible, tokenized value—be it food, labor, resources, troops, or even the
trusty donkeys required for transport. These fungible tokens are generated within Eternum, but can be bridged in and out
of the game through Realms or Banks and traded on external marketplaces. All materials have an associated weight, which
determines the amount that can be stored and the number of donkeys required to move the goods from place to place.

Note: This document will use 'materials' to refer to all tangible assets - i.e. food, resources, ancient fragments,
labor, troops, and donkeys. This is not to be confused with 'resources', which specifically refers to the 22 resources
ranging from wood through to dragonhide. Tokens such as $LORDS and $STRK are not considered materials.

## Cost of Production

With the exception of food, nothing in Eternum is produced for free; every unit of production demands an input—food,
labor, resources, or some combination thereof. Lords must consider these costs and effectively balance trade to ensure
they can survive the season.

## Travel

Transporting materials across the vast lands of Eternum takes time… and donkeys. Every transfer of materials between
locations relies exclusively on these speedy and dependable creatures. These donkeys are single-use, consumed upon
completing their journey, making continuous acquisition vital for maintaining trade routes and strategic flexibility.

Moving tokenized troops through the donkey network may be fast, but to dominate your enemies and ensure security of your
lands, troops must be deployed to an army. Once troops are deployed, they cannot be retokenized, and are doomed to their
fate within this season of Eternum. Once troops are deployed to the world map, their mobility is governed by stamina,
limiting the distance they can travel and the number of actions they can perform. Travelling between hexes or initiating
an attack expends valuable stamina points, but they are partially replenished each Eternum Day.

## Hexes

Eternum unfolds across an infinite, procedurally-generated, hexagonal-tile world map. This represents the physical space
of the world and defines spawn locations, biome distribution, and distances between Realms, Villages, and other world
structures. This is the layer in which armies explore, fight, raid and capture, and is explorable in the 'World' view.

Zooming in, each Realm or Village hex is broken up into smaller hexes which define the local area and building placement
(hexes within hexes!). Here in the 'Local' view, Lords can plan out their building placement and closely manage their
population, supply chain, and production.

## Biomes

Each hexagonal tile on the world map possesses a distinct biome. Each troop type responds uniquely to these varied
terrains, gaining or losing combat efficiency and stamina based on their affinity with different biomes. Successful
Lords must master biome knowledge to exploit strategic advantages or avoid costly missteps.

<Biomes />

## The Known World

Initial scouting reports have revealed some key details about the world:

* Six ancient Banks have been located, distributed evenly in a ring around the central point. These structures are
  perfectly situated and designed to facilitate trade in this new world. Sufficiently prepared Lords may be able to
  capture these lucrative structures, but will likely make themselves a target in the process.

* An unknown number of Hyperstructure Foundations are scattered around the unexplored world. These are rare, key
  locations that must be claimed by Lords in order to rebuild the ancient Hyperstructures that are critical to achieving
  victory. They are more likely to be found in the central regions of the map, and as each subsequent Hyperstructure
  Foundation is discovered, the likelihood of finding another one in the world diminishes.

* Hundreds of mines are also strewn across the landscape. Prospectors have theorized that these mines are hotspots for
  finding ancient fragments needed to reconstruct key sections of the Hyperstructures.

* Bandits roam throughout the lands and have established lairs in each of the key locations described above. The newly
  arrived Lords will need to overcome these lawless brigands to bring order and control back to Eternum.

* Mysterious hooded figures lurk in the mist, offering to play a strange card game with any who wish to try their luck.

# World Structures

<SeasonOneArchive />

## Hyperstructures

Hyperstructures are colossal edifices constructed atop ancient foundations hidden across Eternum, representing
monumental achievements and symbols of dominance in the game world. Lords must uncover and claim Hyperstructure
Foundations through exploration and combat, then invest substantial quantities of resources, labor, and ancient
fragments to reconstruct these majestic towers. Successfully completing and controlling Hyperstructures is the primary
path to victory in Eternum, making them ultimate strategic targets for ambitious Lords and powerful tribes alike.

<img src={iconUrl("hyperstructure", 256)} width="256" alt="Hyperstructure" />

### Hyperstructure Foundations

These ancient, indestructible platforms are primarily scattered within the ring of Banks that surround the center of the
map. There is a chance of discovering a Hyperstructure Foundation each time an army explores an unrevealed hex,
calculated using the following formula:

```
Discovery Chance = (0.975^x × y) - (z × 0.001)
```

Where:

* **x** = the number of hexes from the center of the map
* **y** = the base chance of finding a Hyperstructure Foundation on the central hex (4%)
* **z** = the number of Hyperstructure Foundations already discovered

For example, if you're exploring a hex 100 tiles from the center and 5 foundations have already been discovered:

```
Discovery Chance = (0.975^100 × 4) - (5 × 0.001)
                 = (0.079 × 4) - 0.005
                 = 0.316 - 0.005
                 = 0.311 or 31.1%
```

The result of this function means that Hyperstructure Foundations are generally only able to be found within 300 tiles
of the center of the map. As more are found, the chance of finding another diminishes and makes it even less likely to
find one further from the center. Lords are therefore incentivized to explore early in the season to discover these key
components for victory.

Hyperstructure Foundations and Hyperstructures both have four defense slots to deter attacks from rival Lords. However,
like other world structures they are not able to maintain any field armies.

## Fragment Mines

Fragment Mines are rare, ancient nodes scattered throughout the unexplored hexes of Eternum. They produce valuable
ancient fragments, a vital resource needed for constructing Hyperstructures. Once discovered by an exploring field army,
a Fragment Mine must first be claimed by defeating its resident bandit forces. Only then can Lords begin extracting the
mysterious shards hidden within. Each mine contains a finite number of ancient fragments and will permanently cease
production when depleted.

<img src={iconUrl("mine", 256)} width="256" alt="Fragment Mine" />

The chance of discovering a Fragment Mine when exploring an unrevealed hex is static at 1 in 150, or 0.67%. Mines can
contain between 300,000 and 3 million ancient fragments in incremental amounts (i.e. 300k, 600k, 900k…). It will take
some time for the full amount of ancient fragments to be produced by the structure, and the more that the mine contains,
the longer it will take. Lords will need to ensure that their mines are adequately defended from rivals, however they
only have one defense slot to support a guard army.

# Armies

## Deploying

Barracks train Knights at your realm. **Deploy** puts them on a tile beside the realm as an army, in one of its army
slots: 3 for a new realm, up to 6 as its castle rises. Each troop
deployed costs 2 wheat, and each castle level deploys more troops at once
([Your realm](/frontier/realm#the-castle)).

<Shot name="frontier/deploy" frame="phone" caption="Deploying on a phone: how many troops, the stamina the slot gives them, and the wheat it costs." />

## Stamina

An army holds 120 stamina and recovers 1 every
2  minutes, so an empty bar fills in 4 hours. Revealing a
tile costs 20, a step over known ground 10, and an attack needs
30\. Every step and every reveal also costs 0.02 wheat per troop, so a
big army needs farms behind it.

**Refill** tops an army's stamina up at once for 1 LORDS a missing point, paid from your
realm's LORDS. A well gives 60 stamina. Skill points also restore stamina, and Logistics raises an
army's maximum by up to 150.

### Stamina stays with the slot

Stamina belongs to the army slot, not to the army in it. Choose an open slot when deploying.

* A slot's first army of the day starts with a full bar: 120 stamina, plus what your Supply yard's
  Logistics adds.
* When an army dies, is disbanded or merges into another, its slot keeps the stamina it had left, and that stamina keeps
  recovering.
* The next army deployed into that slot starts with that stamina, not a full bar. Replacing a tired army never brings a
  fresh one.
* The new army's own maximum still caps it. Its XP and tiers start again from your realm's training; only the stamina
  carries over.
* When the day ends, every slot is cleared, so each slot's first army tomorrow starts full again.

## Merging and disbanding

Two of your armies standing side by side can merge, keeping the lower of their two staminas. An army you disband loses
its troops; Frontier asks first.

## XP and attributes

An army earns 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 XP per revealed tile at Surface / Ethereal I / Ethereal II / Ethereal III. It also earns XP for cleared guards and 200 XP from a shrine. XP only
increases. At 75 / 200 / 400 / 800 / 1,500 XP it earns Common, Uncommon, Rare, Epic and Legendary skill
points. The same milestones repeat every 1,500 XP.

Each attribute starts at 0, an empty socket. A point raises one attribute one rank, up to the point's own rank. Level up
uses the lowest fitting point you hold. Raising restores 20 / 30 / 40 / 50 / 60 stamina according
to the point spent. Choose Stamina instead to restore double that amount, up to your current maximum.

* **Battle** hits up to 100% harder.
* **Logistics** carries up to 150 more stamina.
* **Scouting** adds 10% / 15% / 20% / 25% / 30% to the chosen kind's find rate at each rank. Putting all
  five ranks into one kind adds 100%.
* **Homecoming** sends a share of survivors home when the day ends
  ([The expedition](/frontier/expedition#the-days-end)).

XP, ranks and unspent points end with the army. A full merge keeps the receiving army's progress and discards the
source's. Training buildings grant new armies rank I, and research raises their starting rank through V
([Your realm](/frontier/realm#research)).

<Shot frame="panel" name="frontier/level-up" caption="An army's panel: its troops, stamina and XP, then the next tier of each attribute." />

## Fights

No dice are rolled in Frontier, so a fight's forecast is its result. Before you attack, a site's card shows each side's
troops before and after, and what clearing it pays. The ground does not matter: no biome helps or hurts either side.

# Chests

Every Frontier chest pays LORDS, from the season's pool, into your realm. There are two: the chest a ruin guards, and
the rare chest a reveal turns up. A chest pays LORDS and nothing else: no item, relic or cosmetic. Until the LORDS
launch, these are test LORDS ([LORDS](/account/lords)).

## A ruin's chest

Clearing a ruin pays its chest, and gives the army XP by the ruin guard's starting strength. The chest's rarity is
rolled when the ruin is found, from the odds of the depth it stands on:

| Ground       | Common | Uncommon | Rare   | Epic | Legendary |
| ------------ | ------ | -------- | ------ | ---- | --------- |
| Surface      | 50%    | 27%      | 14%    | 6%   | 3%        |
| Ethereal I   | 0%     | 50%      | 28.33% | 15%  | 6.67%     |
| Ethereal II  | 0%     | 0%       | 55%    | 30%  | 15%       |
| Ethereal III | 0%     | 0%       | 0%     | 64%  | 36%       |

A chest is worth its tier's shares at the day's price:

| Tier   | Common | Uncommon | Rare | Epic | Legendary |
| ------ | ------ | -------- | ---- | ---- | --------- |
| Shares | 1      | 2        | 4    | 10   | 20        |

The price of a share is set each day from the season's pool, and never exceeds  50
LORDS. The Season page shows the pool, today's price and its ceiling. A ruin shows its tier and LORDS before you fight.
Its LORDS are reserved and paid only when you clear it. If today's available budget cannot cover it, the clear refuses
without spending the attack. You can reveal any number of ruins, then choose one to clear: your other ruins disappear,
across every depth, and your realm finds no more ruins until the next day.

<Shot frame="panel" name="frontier/season-pool" caption="The Season board: the pool, what today releases, a share's price and its ceiling." />

## A chest in the wild

Each newly revealed surface tile has a 1 in 10,000 chance of a chest, found only while
the day's budget can fund it. It is always common: one share at today's price, set aside as it is found. Open it with
one of your armies beside it before the day ends; its LORDS go to that army's realm.

<Video frame="wide" name="frontier/open-chest" caption="A chest found in the wild, opened by the army beside it." />

## Your LORDS

Your realm's LORDS pay for Refill, and you can withdraw them to your payout wallet until the season's claim window
closes, 1 hour short of 7 days after the season ends.

# Frontier

Frontier is free to play. Every day a new map opens around your realm. Your armies explore it, clear what guards it, and
send home what they find. No other player can attack you.

<Video name="frontier/realm-tiers" caption="Your realm grows as its castle rises, and is raised again on every new map with all it holds." />

## The day

1. **Deploy.** Barracks train troops at your realm. Deploying puts them on a tile beside it as an army.
2. **Explore.** The map is hidden until you walk it. What sits on a tile is decided the moment an army reveals it, so
   nobody can scout ahead. Each tile an army reveals sends Essence or labor home.
3. **Clear.** Trolls, rifts, ruins and beasts guard their sites. Each shows its guard and what it pays before you
   attack.
4. **Bring it home.** Everything a reveal or a clear pays goes straight to your realm. An army never carries anything
   worth losing.

A day lasts 8 to 24 hours. The lengths are drawn for the whole
season in bags of 5 days, one of each length, 80 hours a bag. No bag opens
with the shortest day, and two shortest days never follow each other. The top bar shows when today ends and how long
tomorrow lasts.

When the day ends, the map and every army on it are gone. Your realm is raised again on the next map with everything it
holds.

| Gone when the day ends            | Kept by your realm                   |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| The map and its fog               | Its buildings, castle and research   |
| Every Troll, rift, ruin and beast | Its troops, wheat, labor and Essence |
| Every army you sent out           | Its LORDS                            |

## What you can do

* Found your realm for free the first time you enter, on any day of the season.
* Build farms, barracks, workshops, workers' huts and four training buildings, and raise your castle.
* Research each building type up to legendary, then shrines, wells and the three Ethereal depths.
* Field 3 armies of Knights, and up to 6 as the castle rises.
* Explore, move, and fight the guards of Trolls, rifts, ruins and beasts.
* Earn skill points with XP, then spend them on Battle, Logistics, Scouting, Homecoming or Stamina.
* Use a shrine or a well beside one of your armies.
* Open chests, Refill stamina with LORDS, and withdraw LORDS to your payout wallet.
* Merge two of your armies standing side by side, or disband one.
* Claim labor each day for every Realm in your payout wallet.
* Visit another player's realm from the Season board.

## What you can't do

* Fight another player, or be attacked by one. Your realm has no guard slots because nothing can reach it.
* Trade, bridge, or send anything to another player.
* Raise Crossbowmen, Paladins, or troops above T1.
* Keep the map or your armies after the day ends.
* Use relics, join a tribe, pledge faith, or build a Hyperstructure.
* Win or lose a fight by luck: no dice are rolled, so a fight's forecast is its result.

<Shot name="frontier/hud" caption="Frontier on a desktop: your realm and its armies on the left, the feed on the right, the minimap below." />

## Your first day

Your realm is founded the first time you enter the Frontier season, and from then on it is raised again on every new
map, in its own stretch of wilderness. Entry is free and open for the whole season, so a player who arrives late starts
the same way as one who arrived on day one.

A new realm starts with 1,500 troops, 4,500 wheat and 2,000 labor.
Build barracks, deploy an army, and walk it into the fog.

# Damage

<SeasonOneArchive />

import { DamageCalculation } from "@/components/DamageCalculation";

## Damage Overview

Damage is defined as the number of casualties dealt by one army against the other in an instance of combat. When combat
is initiated (i.e. one army attacks another), damage for both the attacking and defending army is calculated based on a
damage formula that considers troop numbers, troop tier, biome effects, stamina penalties, and battle timer penalties.

## Combat Process

When an army attacks another from an adjacent tile, the following sequence occurs:

1. **Stamina Assessment**: The attacker must have sufficient stamina to engage in combat, the defender must have enough
   to avoid a low-stamina damage penalty
2. **Damage Calculation**: Damage is calculated for both combatants using the damage formula
3. **Casualty Application**: Damage is applied simultaneously to both armies
4. **Battle Timers**: Each combatant receives a battle timer, which prevents them from initiating new attacks while
   active.
5. **Refunds**: Damage-ratio refunds are calculated and applied to battle timers and stamina

## Damage Calculation

<DamageCalculation />

## Battle Timers

Immediately after damage is applied, each combatant receives a **battle timer** that prevents it from attacking an army
or Realm until the timer expires. Armies are free to take any other action while the timer is active. The base duration
is the length of a single Phase (ten minutes in Eternum). Timers for different armies are maintained independently of
the world clock.

If an army that currently has a battle timer is attacked by another army, it will deal 15% less damage during that
combat round. This reduction is represented by the battle timer penalty modifier (T) in the damage equation and reflects
the army's reduced combat readiness while recovering from previous engagements.

## Damage-Ratio Refunds

The combat system includes a refund mechanism designed to prevent smaller, weaker armies from indefinitely stalling
larger forces. When combat is significantly imbalanced, the dominant army receives:

* **Stamina Refunds**: Based on the damage ratio between armies
* **Battle Timer Reduction**: Shorter cooldowns for overwhelming victories

### Refund Calculation

The system calculates a **Refund Factor** based on the damage ratio between armies **x** and **y**:

* **Damage Ratio (R<sub>x</sub>)**: **x**'s damage ÷ **y**'s damage

* **Refund Factor f(R<sub>x</sub>)**: determines the amount of stamina refunded and battle timer reduction

* **If R<sub>x</sub> ≥ 10**: f(R<sub>x</sub>) = 1 (maximum refund)

* **If R<sub>x</sub> ≤ 2.5**: f(R<sub>x</sub>) = 0 (no refund)

* **If 2.5 \< R<sub>x</sub> \< 10**: f(R<sub>x</sub>) = (R<sub>x</sub> - 2.5) ÷ (10 - 2.5)

For army **x**:

* **Refunded Stamina**: (combat cost) × f(R<sub>x</sub>), rounded up
* **Battle Timer Length**: (base timer) × \[1 - f(R<sub>x</sub>)]

The same calculations are applied for army **y**

# Raiding

<SeasonOneArchive />

Raiding allows field armies to attempt theft of valuable resources directly from an adjacent target structure, without
first having to battle through enemy guard armies. Lords can direct their armies to complete this action by selecting
the 'raid' action when attacking an enemy structure.

![Raiding interface showing an army preparing to raid a structure](/screenshots/raiding.png)

## Raid Requirements

Before attempting a raid, your army must meet the following requirements:

* Have at least **50** stamina
* Be adjacent to the target structure
* Structure must contain raidable resources

## Raidable Materials

Raidable materials are stolen based on rarity, with the most valuable taken first. Rarer materials are prioritized
during raids:

<RaidableResources />

<div style={importantNote.container}>
  <h3 style={importantNote.title}>⚠️ IMPORTANT NOTE</h3>
  <p style={importantNote.content}>No materials are safe from pillagers, $LORDS tokens can be raided as well</p>
</div>

## Raid Success

The success chance of a raid is determined by the following conditions:

| Condition                                                                  | Raid Success Chance                           |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Raiding army's total damage \< **50%** of combined guard armies' damage    | **0%**                                        |
| Raiding army's total damage > **200%** of combined guard armies' damage    | **100%**                                      |
| Raiding army's damage between **50%** and **200%** of guard armies' damage | Scales proportionally from **0%** to **100%** |
| No guard armies present                                                    | **100%** (automatic success)                  |

Upon a successful raid, resources are transferred directly to the raiding army's inventory up to its carrying capacity.
It is only possible to steal food, resources, and ancient fragments from a raid, tokens such as $LORDS and $STRK can
only be stolen from another player by claiming a Realm into which tokens have been bridged or transferred.

## Damage in Raids

At the conclusion of a raid, regardless of whether it was successful or not, damage is calculated for both the raiding
army and the guard armies. This damage is significantly reduced by a raid damage modifier, simulating the concept of a
pillaging and looting event as opposed to a full-scale battle. The raid damage modifier reduces damage to
**10%** of that which would be applied in an attack
action.

Damage from a raiding action is calculated according to the following rules:

* The raiding army troop total is split evenly across each guard army.
* A standard combat damage calculation is then applied to each combat instance.
* Each damage number is multiplied by the raid damage modifier before casualties are applied.

### Example:

Consider **1000** raiders attacking a structure with two occupied guard slots:

* Slot 1: **400** troops
* Slot 2: **600** troops

The raiding army is split into two groups of **500** troops, each attacking one guard slot. Assume that the damage
calculations result in the following:

* Raiding army deals **180** damage to Slot 1 and **150** damage to Slot 2 = **330** total damage
* Guard armies deal **120** damage from Slot 1 and **200** damage from Slot 2 = **320** total damage.

Applying the raid damage modifier, actual losses become:

* Guards: Slot 1 loses **36** troops (**10%** of 180),
  Slot 2 loses **30** troops (**10%** of 150).
* Raiders: Lose **64** troops total (**10%** of 320).

## Raid Outcomes

After a raid attempt, one of two outcomes will occur:

1. **Successful Raid**
   * Resources are transferred to your army's inventory
   * Resources are limited by your army's carrying capacity
   * Both armies suffer reduced casualties (**10%** of
     normal combat damage)
   * Your army's stamina is reduced by **50**

2. **Failed Raid**
   * No resources are stolen
   * Both armies still suffer reduced casualties (**10%**
     of normal combat damage)
   * Your army's stamina is still reduced by **50**

The outcome is determined by comparing your army's total damage output against the combined damage output of all guard
armies present. If no guard armies are present, the raid automatically succeeds.

# Stamina & Biome Effects

<SeasonOneArchive />

## Stamina

Stamina is a vital resource governing the mobility and effectiveness of armies. Each deployed army possesses a maximum
stamina value determined by its troop type and tier as explained above. Field armies expend stamina when moving,
exploring unknown hexes, or engaging in combat against other armies and structures. Guard armies only expend stamina
when attacking armies in hexes adjacent to their structure.

## Daily Phases and Stamina

Every Eternum Day is divided into six equal phases: early hours, dawn, morning, afternoon, dusk, and late evening.
Stamina is granted instantly when a new Phase begins, and no other automatic stamina regeneration occurs.

**Eternum Stamina System:**

* **Day Length**: 6 minutes (1 minute per phase)
* **Stamina Regeneration per Phase**: 20 stamina
* **Total Stamina Regeneration per Day**: 120 stamina

## Combat Stamina Mechanics

### Attacking Army

The attacking army is the army initiating an instance of combat. To begin a combat round, the attacker must spend
**50 stamina**. If the attacker does not have the full stamina cost, the attack command is
rejected and no combat occurs.

### Defending Army

The defending army is the army being attacked in an instance of combat. The defender simultaneously spends
**40 stamina**. If the defender's current stamina is below this required threshold, they
receive a **30% damage penalty** during that combat round.

## Biomes (Combat Effects)

The combat effectiveness of different troop types is impacted by the biome on which the defending army is located when
engaging in battle. Armies can gain either a combat advantage (**+30%** damage), disadvantage (**-30%** damage), or no
effect, depending on their affinity with a particular biome. To summarise these combat modifiers for each troop type:

⚔️️ ️️️**Knights** excel in forests, leveraging cover and mobility, but suffer reduced effectiveness in deserts, beaches,
and snow-covered regions where their heavy armour and weapons can make it difficult to move on the soft ground cover.
Knights are equally advantaged, disadvantaged or not affected across all biome types.

🏹 **Crossbowmen** benefit from open seas and unstable terrains (such as beaches and snow), where they can remain static
and make the most of their ranged capabilities. However, they will be disadvantaged in flat, open terrain with no cover
or opportunity for concealment. Crossbowmen are the least advantaged, but also the least disadvantaged by biome
modifiers of the three troop types.

🐴 ️**Paladins** dominate open, flat terrain where mounted units thrive, but are hindered in dense forests and on open
water where their mounted capabilities are negated. They are the most advantaged, but also the most disadvantaged troop
type. In combination with the biome effects on stamina use, Paladins are less versatile than other Knights or
Crossbowmen, but are devastatingly effective in the right situations.

**Note: The scorched biome applies +30% damage to all troop types, representing the additional casualties sustained from
battling in an inhospitable environment.**

# Buildings

<SeasonOneArchive />

## Standard vs. Simple

Season 1 offers two building systems. Lords can switch between standard and simple building modes at any time by
clicking the toggle at the top of the construction tab.

![Simple Mode Production](/gifs/simple-mode.gif)

Note: Some buildings only require food to construct. Farms, Fishing Villages and Resource Buildings have the same
construction cost in both standard and simple building modes.

### Standard Mode

This is the classic Eternum experience. Construction requires a combination of labor and a particular set of resources
as building materials. Standard mode is more complex as it involves a greater number of materials in storage, however it
is far more efficient overall. This building mode is generally designed for those with multiple Realms and/or Villages
working with economies of scale.

### Simple Mode

This mode allows players to utilize Labor, a new material for Season 1, as the singular input for construction. Simple
mode greatly improves accessibility and simplifies gameplay overall, but is much less efficient in both time and
materials. This would be the preferred system for players with smaller holdings and limited access to a variety of
resources.

## Placement & Population

### Placement

The construction tab can be accessed in the Local view. Buildings can be constructed on any buildable hex of a Realm or
Village, provided the owner has the requisite materials and population capacity. Buildable hexes are defined by the
upgrade level (i.e. Settlement, City, Kingdom, or Empire) and are visible as hexes with patches of bare, prepared earth.
Most buildings can be constructed in either simple or standard mode, which toggled at the top of the construction tab.

![Building Placement](/gifs/construction.gif)

### Population

Population is the total population utilised by the currently constructed production buildings on a Realm or Village,
while the population capacity is the maximum available population with which to construct production buildings. Worker
Huts can be built to increase population capacity, while all other buildings require population capacity to be available
in order to construct. Lords must balance their construction to ensure there is enough population capacity available to
construct their desired production buildings.

the Keep. This structure represents your seat of power and the central point of governance, and facilitates key
functions such as Realm/Village upgrades and Labor production. This building cannot be destroyed and provides a baseline
population capacity of 5.

### Same Building Cost Increase

Building multiple buildings of the same type will cost progressively more each time. The cost increase is calculated
using the following formula:

```
Cost = Base Cost + (Base Cost × 0.5 × (N-1)²)
```

Where:

* **Base Cost** is the initial cost of the building (from the tables above)
* **N** is the number of the same buildings already constructed

For example, if you build a second building of the same type (N=2), the cost will be:

```
Cost = Base Cost + (Base Cost × 0.5 × (2-1)²)
     = Base Cost + (Base Cost × 0.5 × 1)
     = Base Cost × 1.5
```

Note: Buildings need to be exactly the same type for this rule to apply. For example, building a Stone Quarry after a
Lumber Camp (both being resource buildings) would not be subject to this cost increase.

# Resource Automation

<SeasonOneArchive />

Resource automation allows players to set up continuous production processes and automated transfers within their realms
and between entities. This system helps manage resource generation and distribution efficiently without constant manual
intervention.

## Overview

The automation system works by processing two types of "orders" that you define:

1. **Production Orders**: Automate resource production within your realms
2. **Transfer Orders**: Automate resource transfers between your entities (realms, villages, hyperstructures, etc.)

Both types of orders specify what you want to automate, the method or conditions, target amounts or schedules, and
priorities.

**Key Features:**

* **Order-Based**: Define specific production and transfer tasks.
* **Priority System**: Control the sequence of execution for multiple orders.
* **Multiple Automation Types**: Choose between production automation and transfer automation.
* **Entity-Specific**: Automation is configured on a per-entity basis.
* **Browser Dependent**: Automation processes run in your browser; the game tab must remain open for automation to
  function.
* **10-Minute Cycles**: All automation is processed every minute.

## How It Works

The automation system periodically checks and processes active orders for each entity.

1. **Processing Interval**: All orders are evaluated every 1 minute.
2. **Order Prioritization**: For each entity, orders are sorted by their priority number (1 being the highest).
3. **Pause Functionality**: You can pause all automation for any entity, which will skip all orders for that entity.
4. **Order Processing**: The system processes different order types:

### Production Order Processing

* **Balance Check**: Before executing, the system checks if the realm has sufficient input resources or labor.
* **Recipe-Based Production**: Production follows predefined recipes from the game's configuration.
* **Cycle Calculation**: Determines the maximum number of production cycles possible based on available inputs.
* **Transaction**: If conditions are met, a blockchain transaction is initiated to perform the production.

### Transfer Order Processing

* **Schedule Check**: For recurring transfers, checks if enough time has passed since the last transfer.
* **Threshold Check**: For conditional transfers, checks if resource levels meet the specified conditions.
* **Resource Availability**: Verifies the source entity has sufficient resources for the transfer.
* **Transaction**: If conditions are met, initiates a transfer transaction between entities.

## Production Types

There are three types of production automation:

1. **Standard (Resource-to-Resource)**:
   * Converts one or more input resources into a different output resource.
   * Example: Using Wood and Stone to produce Tools.
2. **Simple (Labor-to-Resource)**:
   * Utilizes Labor from the realm to produce a specific resource.
   * Example: Using Labor to harvest Food.
3. **Resource-to-Labor**:
   * Converts a specified resource into Labor for the realm.
   * Example: Burning Food to generate Labor.

## Production Order Modes

Production orders can operate in two different modes:

1. **Produce Once**:
   * Produces resources until the target amount is reached, then stops.
   * Shows progress as "Produced / Target" (e.g., "1,500 / 5,000").
   * Can be set to "Infinite" to produce continuously without a limit.

2. **Maintain Balance**:
   * Keeps resource balance at the target level.
   * Production triggers when balance drops below target minus buffer percentage.
   * Example: Target 10,000 with 10% buffer = production starts when balance drops below 9,000.
   * Useful for maintaining steady resource levels.

## Transfer Types

Transfer automation allows you to automatically move resources between your entities. There are three transfer modes:

1. **Recurring Transfers**:
   * Transfers resources at regular intervals.
   * Minimum interval: 1 minute (due to automation cycle).
   * Example: Transfer 1,000 Food from Realm A to Village B every 2 hours.
   * *\[GIF placeholder: Setting up a recurring transfer]*

2. **Maintain Stock Transfers**:
   * Transfers when the destination entity falls below a threshold.
   * Example: Transfer 500 Wood to Village when it has less than 1,000 Wood.
   * Helps keep important entities well-supplied.
   * *\[GIF placeholder: Setting up a maintain stock transfer]*

3. **Depletion Transfers**:
   * Transfers when the source entity exceeds a threshold.
   * Example: Transfer excess Stone when Realm has more than 5,000 Stone.
   * Prevents resource overflow and distributes surplus.
   * *\[GIF placeholder: Setting up a depletion transfer]*

## Managing Automation Orders (UI)

You can manage automation orders through the "Automation" tab within any entity's interface, or through the global "All
Automations" view.

### Production Automation

Production automation is managed within individual realm interfaces.

*\[GIF placeholder: Navigating to realm automation tab]*

#### Adding a New Production Order

1. Click the "Add New Automation" button.
2. Configure the following:
   * **Order Mode**:
     * `Produce Once`: Produce up to target amount then stop
     * `Maintain Balance`: Keep resource balance at target level
   * **Production Type**:
     * `Standard (Resource-based)`
     * `Simple (Labor-based)`
     * `Resource to Labor`
   * **Priority (1-9)**:
     * Set the execution priority. `1` is the highest priority, `9` is the lowest.
   * **Resource to Produce / Resource Input for Labor**:
     * Select the resource you want to produce or consume.
     * The UI shows required input resources for Resource-to-Resource recipes.
   * **Target Amount**:
     * For "Produce Once": Set total quantity to produce, or check "Infinite" for continuous production.
     * For "Maintain Balance": Set the balance level to maintain.
   * **Buffer Percentage** (Maintain Balance only):
     * Production starts when balance drops below target minus buffer percentage.
3. Click "Add Automation".

### Transfer Automation

Transfer automation allows you to move resources between any of your entities.

   * Choose entity type (Your Realms, Your Villages, etc.)

## Important Considerations

### General Requirements

* **Browser Must Be Open**: Automation processing occurs in your browser. If you close the game tab or your browser,
  automation will stop.
* **10-Minute Processing Cycle**: All automation is processed every minute. This affects timing for all orders.
* **Transaction Costs**: Each successful automation cycle that results in production or transfer will involve a
  blockchain transaction, which incurs network transaction fees.
* **Processing Time**: Due to the 10-minute processing interval and potential network conditions, there might be a delay
  before an order is executed or its results are visible.

### Production Automation Considerations

* **Resource Availability**: Production orders will only execute if the required input resources are available in the
  realm at the time of processing.
* **Recipe Dependencies**: Production follows predefined game recipes. You cannot produce resources that don't have
  valid recipes.
* **Storage Limits**: Produced resources will increase your realm's balance and may cause resource loss if storage is
  full.

### Transfer Automation Considerations

* **Entity Ownership**: You can only set up transfers between entities you own.
* **Transfer Timing**:
  * Minimum effective interval is 1 minute due to processing cycle
  * Intervals shorter than 1 minute will effectively transfer every minute
  * Non-10-minute multiples may have irregular timing based on when transfers align with processing cycles
* **Resource Availability**: Transfers will only execute if the source entity has sufficient resources at processing
  time.
* **Multiple Resources**: You can transfer multiple different resources in a single transfer order.
* **Conditional Logic**: Maintain Stock and Depletion transfers check conditions each cycle and only transfer when
  thresholds are met.

### Best Practices

1. **Start Simple**: Begin with basic production orders or simple recurring transfers to understand the system.
2. **Monitor Initially**: Watch your first few automation cycles to ensure orders work as expected.
3. **Use Priorities**: Set appropriate priorities when you have multiple orders to control execution sequence.
4. **Plan for Delays**: Account for the 10-minute processing cycle when planning time-sensitive operations.
5. **Check Resource Flows**: Regularly verify that your automated transfers are maintaining desired resource
   distributions.

# Transfers & Trade

<SeasonOneArchive />

## Transport

### Transfers

Throughout the season, Lords will face the need to transfer materials from place to place around the world map, whether
it be to their own Realms and Villages, or other structures like Banks, Hyperstructures, and Fragment Mines. Lords have
two primary options for initiating transfers:

* **Detailed Transfer**: Players can use the comprehensive transfer menu accessible from the Trade tab. This allows
  transfers of multiple types of materials simultaneously, to any structure on the map—including those belonging to
  other players.

![Detailed Transfer](/gifs/transfer-all.gif)

* **Quick Transfer**: Alternatively, Lords can swiftly transfer resources between their own structures using the
  resources sidebar, ideal for managing internal logistics and maintaining supply chains.

![Quick Transfer](/gifs/transfer-own.gif)

### The Donkey Network

When a transfer is initiated, donkeys travel through the invisible and immutable donkey network. This is the key
infrastructure upon which all material transport takes place, facilitating trade between players and Banks. One donkey
is capable of carrying up to 500 kg of materials, so the number of donkeys required for a transfer depends on the total
weight of the materials being moved. Once a transfer is initiated, it cannot be cancelled, and the journey will take
some time depending on the distance to the destination structure. Upon arrival, transferred materials and donkeys must
be manually claimed via the 'Resource Arrivals' tab before they become available for use.

  <h3 style={importantNote.title}>⚠️ IMPORTANT NOTE</h3>

  <p style={importantNote.content}>
    In Season 1, resource arrivals are restricted by 'gates' to assist in reducing the load that resource arrival
    entities have on the game. As donkeys arrive at their destination bearing their various goods, they need to wait
    until the start of a new Eternum Day for the structure's arrival gate to open before they can enter and be claimed
    by the owning Lord. For example, if a transfer arrives at 2:56 p.m. the owner of the structure will only need to

## Banks & Marketplaces

### Banks

<div style={{ display: "flex", justifyContent: "center", margin: "2rem 0" }}>
  <img src={iconUrl("bank", 256)} width="256" alt="Bank" style={{ maxWidth: "100%", height: "auto" }} />
</div>

Six ancient Banks stand evenly distributed around, and equidistant from, the center of the map.

At the start of the game, each Bank is occupied by powerful groups of bandits. Lords who conquer a Bank become the
beneficiary of any fees from the Automated Market Maker (AMM) trades that they facilitate. Although Banks collect fees
independently, they collectively share liquidity, ensuring consistent and stable trading conditions throughout Eternum.
Realms and Villages automatically utilize their nearest Bank when trading through the AMM.

### AMM (Automated Market Maker)

The Automated Market Maker (AMM) is a decentralized trading protocol facilitated by the six Banks, allowing for
instantaneous and trustless trades 24/7—the DeFi of Eternum. Trades executed via the AMM automatically utilize the
closest Bank, directing trade fees specifically to that Bank's owner. Lords can further benefit from the AMM by
contributing materials and $LORDS to liquidity pools, earning a share of the fees generated from subsequent swaps. Lords
should be aware of the relatively high fees for using the AMM and consider the Orderbook for other trading options
first.

### Orderbook

Complementing the AMM, the Orderbook is a peer-to-peer marketplace, allowing Lords to place precise buy or sell orders
at chosen quantities and prices. This traditional method of trading incurs lower fees and offers Lords strategic
control, enabling them to manage market conditions to their advantage and set OTC orders for friends and allies.

## Stamina

Troops in Eternum are deployed onto the world map as armies. Army movement and actions across the map are governed by
stamina, a critical resource Lords must carefully manage.

Stamina is expended whenever an army moves between hexes, explores previously unrevealed areas, or launches an attack
against enemy forces. Each of these actions has a specific stamina cost, making strategic planning essential to
maximizing an army's effectiveness. Armies regenerate stamina at a consistent rate of +20
stamina per Phase (1 minute), totaling +120 stamina per
Eternum Day. Each troop type possesses different maximum stamina capacities, outlined in the table below:

<TroopMovementTable />

Lords should remain cautious about fully depleting the stamina of their armies, as forces with low stamina may find
themselves severely disadvantaged if attacked by enemy forces.

## Exploring

When an army is positioned adjacent to an unexplored hex, Lords may choose to send that army forth to uncover what lies
hidden. Exploring an unknown hex always costs 30 stamina and will reveal that hex to all
Lords, permanently expanding the known boundaries of the world.

If the newly explored hex contains a world structure or an agent army, the exploring army will automatically be pushed
back to the hex from which they started without any additional stamina penalty. Additionally, during exploration, armies
gather any valuables they encounter, receiving a stack of randomly selected resources directly into their inventory.

**Relic Chest Discovery**: Each exploration also triggers a global check for **Relic Chest** discovery. If enough time
has passed since the last chest was discovered globally, a new chest will spawn at a calculated distance from the
exploration point. This creates exciting opportunities for explorers to trigger discoveries that benefit the entire game
world.

If an army becomes overloaded, they will not be able to undertake further exploration, but can continue to travel on
previously-explored hexes. Armies can return to a structure to offload the materials, or drop them to free up space.
Note that any materials discarded in the field will be permanently lost (burned).

Given the possibility of encountering hostile agent armies during exploration, Lords should ensure that their exploring
armies are well-prepared and capable of defending themselves in unexpected combat situations.

## Troop Transfers

Lords may transfer troops from one army to another, provided that they are on adjacent hexes, the same troop type, and
the same tier. To transfer troops from one army to another, or to a structure, the player first needs to select its army
on the worldmap using left-click, then right-click on the target and select the Transfer -> Transfer Troops tab. Troops
can be transferred between armies (and Realm defense slots) at any time, and transfers are not impacted by battle
timers. However, in all instances of a troop transfer, the army that has received troops:

* adopts the **lower** of the two armies' stamina values, and
* adopts the **higher** of the two armies' battle timers.

## An Unexplored World

At the beginning of each season, Eternum lies entirely unexplored, shrouded in mystery and opportunity. The only
exceptions are the six ancient Banks, whose location and surrounding hexes are known and visible from the outset. They
can be seen when settling and when observing the world view, each equidistant from the center of the map and from one
another.

When Lords settle a Realm, the six hexes directly adjacent to the Realm hex and the six possible village locations are
automatically revealed if they haven't already been explored. Beyond these instances, the world remains cloaked by the
mist, waiting for ambitious Lords to send forth their armies to explore. Once a hex is explored, the state of the hex
(i.e. the biome and whether it contains a world structure or army) is permanently revealed to all Lords.

## Biomes

Each hex is assigned one of 16 biome types, each with unique aesthetics and strategic implications. These biomes are
procedurally generated across the world map following specific rules that dictate their distribution, ensuring a
coherent yet varied world, with certain biomes naturally occurring more or less frequently than others.

Biomes impact both combat effectiveness and troop movement. Each troop type—Knights, Crossbowmen, and Paladins—can
potentially be advantaged or disadvantaged in battle depending on the biome in which combat takes place; this is
explained in more detail in the [Military section](/eternum/military/stamina-and-biomes). Additionally, biomes have the
potential to enhance or impede army maneuvers, as the various biomes impact the stamina consumption of each troop type
differently as they move between hexes—this is explained further in the
[Movement & Exploration](/eternum/worldmap-movement/movement) section below.
