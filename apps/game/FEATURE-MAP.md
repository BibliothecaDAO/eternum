# Feature map — game client

Relic crates on the Blitz map: select the chest's tile to focus its labelled crate and opening action. A sealed crate
says "Three relics. Open to reveal them."; its actual relics appear on opening and stay in the tile's receipt. Open
needs an adjacent own army (the panel otherwise says, in a plain line where Open would stand, "Move one of your armies
next to the crate to open it."). `/lab/relic-chest` exercises that single presentation using the production instanced
model, opening pool, panel and native opening story. Run
`pnpm --dir apps/game prove:relic-chest --port 4199 --out <fresh-dir>` for both renderer captures and behavior proof.

Next-season research proposals: `/lab/data?mode=frontier&view=balance` (Data → Balance & data → Research balance). What
players never use, ranked from prod's counts with a HUD screenshot beside each: `/lab/data/usage` (Data → UI usage, the
Game switch picks Blitz or Frontier); every number is the newest live-data pull's:
`apps/game/scripts/live-data/live-data.py all`, run from the folder that keeps the pulls, writes the pull's `usage.json`
(read at `/live-data/usage.json`; stills at `/live-data/usage-shots`). **Storage** compares deleting storage research
with smaller late-game base stores; pick a realm and plan a Farm or Workshop upgrade. **Research paths** compares
separate side levels, a shared four-purchase budget and the current rule; start with Drill level 2 and try Drill level 3
or Rations level 2. **Beast labor** compares three guard-based clear payouts at every depth and replays 28 recorded
clears. Plans are local fixtures and send no game commands. Serve the client on an assigned port, open the route with
`&evidence=/absolute/new/capture/directory` (use `?` without another query), then run
`scripts/frontier/prove-research-balance.playwright.js` through the Playwright connection with the page as its argument.
It clicks the proposals, verifies results and captures desktop and phone; keep its returned transcript with the images.
No gameplay or preset rule changes are part of this study.

Frontier exploration has a 1/10,000 chance per newly revealed surface tile to find a funded reward chest. Select its
labelled map tile; an adjacent own living army can Open it. The panel shows its reserved common-share LORDS amount and
day-rollover expiry; opening credits the army’s home realm and leaves a receipt in Frontier’s selection sheet, including
when the opened tile is selected again. The proof command above enters the real Frontier `/g/<chain>/<game>/map` route
and its mounted HUD, selects through map clicks, and checks missing metadata, amount/expiry, Open, the retained receipt
and the expired reservation on the controlled lab calendar before entering the live world. The live game clock is never
advanced by this proof. The selection-sheet tests cross the exact clock rollover. Route proof uses local reward facts, a
fixture release catalogue and a non-signing command transport; it does not prove a live payout. Blitz retains its visual
lab proof. Both renderer backends are checked. The route waits for the mounted Guided rail and canvas; it frames the
chest through the minimap's Position bridge before clicking its tile. `/lab/relic-chest?mode=frontier` holds its clock
until **Advance to day rollover**; **Reset chest** restores the funded time.

Frontier fight cards and Events share **Victory**, **Army lost** and **Guard holds**. Events, the Log and Today name a
site by its one name, the map label's (and a rename): "clientlane2 vs Ashford · Victory", "Ashford: Victory, lost 84,
guard 154 to 0", "Ashford cleared", "Ashford found"; its guard's species is the fight's forces ("507 T1 Knight vs 154
Wraith") and a find's kind. A roaming beast's card is titled by its species; the fight result retains that species'
portrait after the guard clears. Today writes the XP each line moved: "+79 labor, +2 XP" (a reveal), "Ashford cleared,
+82 XP" (a clear, from the guard the site started with), "Battle Uncommon, −100 XP" (a level-up's price). A Shrine's 200
XP and the XP an emptied army takes with it in a merge leave no story, so no line. Dev: `/lab/kit/hud/today` (the
owner's 22 October day, through the log's own lines). A Frontier founding line names the settler without the realm's
off-map settlement coordinate.

Every link inside a match (a Feed or Log row, a toast, the standings, the Season's Visit, a chat coordinate, Next up's
camp) opens its place through `play/navigation/open-place.ts`: the address carries the normalized hex
(`/g/<chain>/<game>/map?col=&row=`, a Frontier realm's about −1.4 billion), the page is never loaded again, and the map
selects what the link names (the tile, its structure or army). A Frontier story from a day that has closed is no link
(`linkFrom`): the map is built again each day, so its place is not on today's map. Frontier's realms stand millions of
hexes apart, past the floating origin's 8,192-hex reach; the map then draws the world again around the new place in the
same page. The spectator playtest presses a Feed story and visits the board's second and third realms in turn
(`open-feed-event`, `visit-realm`).

## Shell (outside a game)

On a desktop, every page stands on its painting (the age or mode it shows, else the app's landscape) under the app
sheet's bar: the lockup, Play, Season, What's new and Market with their icons, the page's notice, and the player (name
and portrait, or You) at its right end. `Esc` goes back and `Enter` takes the page's step, neither key printed on its
button (`shell/frame/page-frame.tsx`, `shell/frame/tabs.tsx`, `shell/frame/desktop-keys.ts`, the sheet's `AppTopBar` and
`AppPageHead` in `ui/design-system/kit/app-elements.tsx`). A full-screen step (sign-in, a lobby) keeps the bar with the
lockup alone. Every page's content starts at the top, under the bar; a painting is its ground, never space above it.
`Enter` presses the page's step button (the one marked as its step) when it is enabled, nothing has focus that answers
Enter itself, and no dialog is open. On a phone the places are a tab bar at the foot. The app's notice (offline, an
update ready, or Install, which only Play offers) sits in the desktop's top bar before You, and on a phone just above
the foot, one row: Install is its icon, Later and Install; under 1280 wide a notice with action buttons shows its icon
and verbs; a words-only notice keeps its text.

### Season, You and Results

- **Season** shows Frontier's board (its first rows, yours lit or pinned under them) and Blitz's rating board. While a
  Blitz season runs, each paid rating place shows what it would take of the pool if the season ended now; the pool and
  its end stand at the board's title. Once it is over, your share and its **Claim** stand above the rows. The prize is
  read from your own paid games, so signed out the board shows the rating alone. A Frontier row opens that player.
- **You** opens on your own card, your name and portrait from the session (a player not yet known shows a muted bar and
  an empty ring, never a stand-in name), and lists your last 8 finished games in every mode; with none yet, the
  desktop's games plate says they will show there. A row opens its Results, whose **Back** returns to the list (Results
  reached from Play or after a match have **Continue** instead). A session with no name claimed yet offers **Claim
  name** and **Sign out**, on You and in Settings.
- **Results** of a Blitz opens on your place and points, then the game's board and how your points were made ("No points
  scored." when none were). **Share** shares the page itself: its line ("Blitz 02:18 · 1st of 2 · 0 points") and its
  address, through the device's share sheet (a phone), else copied ("Link copied"), else shown to select. A sealed
  chest's **Open** is the page's gold button; **Continue** stands beside it in outline. A reader who did not play sees
  the game (by its name and date, else its number) and the board, and, signed in, that they had no seat.
- **Settings**: **Name** offers **Claim name** once the name differs from the one held; **Devices** lists this device as
  soon as you are signed in. It opens the payout wallet directly when a page sent you to link one
  (`/profile/settings?next=…`) and returns you there once it is linked. Its groups are the app sheet's panels and each
  setting one of their rows (`AppPanel`, `AppSetting`, `ui/design-system/kit/app-elements.tsx`); a row's step (Name,
  Portrait, Payout wallet) opens in a panel under the rows, and a destructive step (sign out, remove a device) asks
  first in the sheet's dialog over the page (`AppDialog`), Keep or Escape its way out.
- **The Blitz lobby** counts down to one moment: entry closing while the slot takes players, then the games' start (the
  lobby's title names the start time).

### Play

- **Now** leads with the one gold step, first match wins: **Enter** a Blitz seat, **Resume** the Frontier day,
  **Results** of a game of yours not yet opened on this device (any mode, looked up in your last 8 finished games),
  **Lobby** of a seat whose game is still being prepared, **Play** (signed out **Play free**, which opens sign-in and
  then the season), **Join** a Blitz, **Watch** one, Eternum's opening. Everything else under way follows, each with its
  own verb.
- **Next and done** shows one mode at a time behind its switch, opening on the first with something to do; a dot marks a
  mode holding something of yours. Blitz: slots to **Join**, a refund to **Take refund**, a seat's **Lobby**,
  **Results**. Eternum: when it opens.
- A read that fails (Blitz's slots, your results) says so with **Try again**; the games that answered stay usable.
- A Blitz lobby, signed out, asks to **Sign in** and returns to it. With no payout wallet, **Link wallet** opens
  Settings on the wallet (`/profile/settings?next=…`) and returns to the lobby once one is linked. The lobby's **Back**
  leads to Play. Sign-in from You or Settings returns to the page it was asked from.
- Play's former addresses, shared publicly (`/blitz`, `/frontier`, `/eternum`, `/dominion`), lead to Play: a permanent
  redirect on a full load (`public/_redirects`), and Play in the app (`RETIRED_PLAY_PATHS` in `src/app.tsx`).

## Blitz and Eternum in a match (`/g/:chain/:game/map` or `/hex`)

A match opens from the lobby (**Your game**) or from home (**Enter**), on the world map in every mode (Frontier too: its
realm is one press of **Realm** away; a spectator's map opens on their own realm when the signed-in player has one in

### Action windows

Full workspaces open in the centre of the desktop; on a phone they use the shared sheet placement. A headed window can
be dragged while open. Closing and reopening it, or replacing it with a different window, starts at its normal placement
again. Re-aiming the same open window at another tile preserves its current drag. Positions are not saved between
openings (`src/ui/design-system/molecules/popover.tsx`).

Use a running client and a private browser profile signed in through the normal sign-in flow:

```sh
pnpm --dir apps/game prove:play --base-url <url> --game <chain>:<game> --profile <private-profile> --tasks boot --out <fresh-dir>
```

The **boot** task enters the game and opens Chat at desktop and phone sizes. It requires the rendered map and Chat, and
fails on page or console errors. It is a signed-in game proof, separate from the signed-out Play/shell fixtures. The
same runner's
`blitz-build-window,blitz-farm,blitz-army,blitz-deploy,blitz-explore,blitz-transfer,blitz-transfer-window,blitz-transfer-gesture,blitz-standings,blitz-standings-detail`
tasks play a seated Blitz player's orders through the HUD (`blitz-standings` also checks the leaderboard's window fits
its table and rows, and at 1440 and 1920 wide that each column header is one title line over one points line, and
`blitz-standings-detail`, at 1920x1080, that a player's detail opens beside the table without stretching it);
`blitz-attack-forecast` (Blitz) and `attack-forecast` (Frontier) open an army's attack forecast on a tile it can attack,
sending nothing, and check its verb stands alone at the right and the rest at the left; and `gate-build,gate-production`
check that nothing acts before play opens (`scripts/game-tasks.mjs`).

For a seated Blitz player with at least two armies able to act:

```sh
pnpm --dir apps/game playtest --base-url <url> --player <email> --chain-id <chain> --game-id <game> --steps blitz-burst --out <fresh-dir>
```

The **blitz-burst** step list cycles armies with Tab and orders reachable hexes by right-click, back to back. Its
verdicts match each click to the send's answer, Herald status and rendered army movement. It uses the shared playtest
runner, which retains transcripts and screenshots and closes what it starts;
[client instructions](AGENTS.md#playtest-the-one-command-that-proves-client-work) describe sign-in, renderer selection,
timing bounds and evidence. The proofs need a live game and eligible player; they do not create accounts or make a
signed-out fixture count as a played match.

## In a match: world map

Watch a running Blitz from Play (**Watch**) to reach the map without signing in; a seated player uses **Enter**.

- **Ownership rings**: select (click or tap) a hex holding a player's structure, theirs or yours, and the hover's pink
  ring lights every hex where that player holds a structure or has an army out. Selecting another hex, or one with no
  player's structure, clears them. They read only the selected hex, and follow captures (to the structure's new owner),
  new and fallen armies, moves and the layer switch (`src/three/scenes/worldmap-ownership-rings.ts`).
- **A selected structure that moves takes the selection with it**: when a Frontier realm is raised on its next day's
  site, the selected hex, its ring, the tile panel and the ownership rings move there together
  (`src/three/scenes/worldmap-selection-follow.ts`).

  zoomed out, `worldmap-content-ladder.ts`), and at rest the label is its token, standing on its thing's label point

### Blitz rules a player meets

- **Entry closes five minutes before the start.** At the close the slot splits into games of up to 24 and the launch
  service seats every player, aiming to finish inside those five minutes; that target is unmeasured for large slots
  (`BLITZ_SLOT_PREPARATION_SECONDS`, `packages/value-ledger/src/blitz-slots.ts`). A game opens at its scheduled time
  once its whole roster is seated; until then its seats show **Lobby** (Preparing). If seating overruns, the game opens
  when its last seat lands and ends at its scheduled end: the start and end never move.
- **Starting forces.** Each of a player's three realms starts with 1,000 T1 troops garrisoned and 1,000 in reserve, one
  realm Knights, one Crossbowmen, one Paladins, in an order drawn per player. No army stands on the map at the start;
  the player creates one from the reserve.
- **Merging armies.** Select an army, then an adjacent army or realm of yours: the transfer panel moves troops between
  them, across realms too. Both sides must hold the same troop type and tier. Frontier opens its **Transfer troops**
  sheet as a merge into the army tapped: the map chose which army goes into which. Its two cards show each army's
  troops, stamina and XP now and after; the army a merge empties is faded with the XP it loses struck out (its upgrades'
  cost included), and the one that stays shows the stamina the merge leaves it. **Merge into …** names the army that
  stays. The one control is how many the picked army sends: typed, or dragged on the slider from 0 to all it holds, each
  end labelled with its amount (a press on an end takes it); it opens at all, the merge, and stops where the tapped army
  is full. To send the other way, pick the other army on the map. If the dissolving army has at least one first-tier
  upgrade more progression (unspent XP plus purchased or trained tiers; 100 XP in the current preset), **Merge into …**
  turns into **Lose N XP, merge into …**, and a second click sends. Smaller gaps send on the first click. Changing the
  amount or army facts clears confirmation. Dev: `/lab/kit/hud/merge`; tests drive the map’s actual selection card.
  Browser proof:
  `pnpm --dir apps/game capture:hud-lab --base-url https://127.0.0.1:<port> --out <new-directory> --only frontier-merge`.
  It takes the loss step and a partial move by the slider, the box and slider holding what is sent, at desktop and phone
  sizes, preserving screenshots and a transcript without sending.

distances, pitch and field of view. `pnpm --dir apps/game prove:beasts <local-url> <fresh-evidence-directory> facing`
drives both zooms, records the rendered headings and camera, and captures the comparisons with idle and mist enabled.

`/lab/models?family=beasts` shows all eight production species, all four ruin depths, standing site compositions and
both roaming beasts. Site, Depth and Beast strength control the real plate and selected card. Select site opens that
card; species portraits are shown at their actual 20px HUD size. The plate has a species title and strength, with no
combat-tier badge. The retired Minotaur, Ghoul, Kappa and Golem deliveries and their old choice routes are removed.

Scene → 100 ruins (`/lab/models?family=beasts&group=site-map&population=100&rendererMode=webgpu-force-webgl`) batches
one hundred complete ruin/guardian compositions through the production models. Measure mist cost takes six alternating
on/off runs, 30 warm-up and 90 sampled frames each, synchronizing WebGL completion. Its result is synchronized frame
cost, not an FPS claim. Scene → 128 beasts (`population=128`) shows sixteen of every species in its production site
composition. Measure idle cost uses the same six-run protocol with mist on throughout. The proof refuses timing on
machine load above 12 or a software renderer.

`pnpm --dir apps/game prove:beasts <local-url> <fresh-evidence-directory>` drives the visible lab controls and records
models, contexts, motion captures, eight HUD portraits, plates and panels. Append `mist` to run the hundred-ruin timing
comparison separately, `idle` for the 128-beast idle comparison, or `site-map webgpu-auto` to repeat the final-map proof
on native WebGPU. `verify:beasts` checks all eight deliveries, budgets and editable-source hashes; `verify:fallen-realm`
checks decoded triangles for zero masonry contacts at each production scale. All authoring sources and paint live in
`asset-sources/frontier-beasts/refined/`; `scripts/frontier-beasts/rebuild.sh <fresh-absolute-directory>` exports those
exact eight packed sources. There is one production path per species and no separate study GLB.
