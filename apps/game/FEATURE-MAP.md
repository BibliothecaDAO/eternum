# Feature map — game client

Play's game doorways, in-game HUD and Guide link use the supported mode catalog (`packages/types/src/words/modes.ts`).
Each supported mode has its own age; an unknown directory mode has no doorway.

Frontier armies live for their recorded allocation day, including after entering another map region. A reused slot keeps
its stamina; the next day starts a fresh slot.

Frontier army management: select an army from the dock or map; on a phone tap its selected token for details. **Delete**
asks for confirmation before removing its troops, carried resources and held Oaths; its slot keeps its stamina. For
**Reinforce**, select the realm and right-click its adjacent army (hold on a phone). Choose the troop count, check the
wheat price, then send. `--steps army-management` deploys ten troops, cancels Delete once, confirms it and checks the
army disappears. `--steps lab-reinforce` records the reinforcement command and resulting balances.

The optional `--steps army-stock` preparation builds a Barracks through the realm board when the saved home needs
reserve troops.

The ruin card (`/lab/kit/hud/site-ruin`) shows the carried relic awarded on victory. Season 1 ruins award relics and
exploration has no cash chests.

Frontier fight cards and Events share **Victory**, **Army lost** and **Guard holds**. Events, the Log and Today name a
site by its one name, the map label's (and a rename): "clientlane2 vs Ashford · Victory", "Ashford: Victory, lost 84,
guard 154 to 0", "Ashford cleared", "Ashford found"; its guard's species is the fight's forces ("507 T1 Knight vs 154
Wraith") and a find's kind. A roaming beast's card is titled by its species; the fight result retains that species'
portrait after the guard clears. The Log shows recorded resource rewards and troop losses, even after the army is gone;
a Log click returns to the recorded tile. Historical events remain readable. A Frontier founding line names the settler
without its off-map settlement coordinate.

Every link inside a match (a Feed or Log row, a toast, the standings, the Season's Visit, Today's camp) opens its place
through `play/navigation/open-place.ts`: the address carries the normalized hex (`/g/<chain>/<game>/map?col=&row=`, a
Frontier realm's about −1.4 billion), the page is never loaded again, and the map selects what the link names (the tile,
its structure or army). A Frontier story from a day that has closed is no link (`linkFrom`): the map is built again each
day, so its place is not on today's map. Frontier's realms stand millions of hexes apart, past the floating origin's
8,192-hex reach; the map then draws the world again around the new place in the same page. The spectator playtest
presses a Feed story and visits the board's second and third realms in turn (`open-feed-event`, `visit-realm`).

## Cleared-site standard

Open `/lab/models?family=cleared-sites` through **Art → Models → Cleared sites**. **Site** compares the shipped Troll or
roaming beasts with the production standard. **View** uses the world camera at gameplay distance 20 or detail
distance 8. Click a model or its selection button to open the reward panel; Close site dismisses it. The selected
standard uses the production receipt component over native clear-result facts, with the Structure and guard removed. In
the game a clear leaves the biome and a walkable tile; the standard and its reward receipt survive an army walking
across it and a reload. The site never becomes a player holding.

Proof: serve the client, open that URL with `&evidence=/absolute/new/capture/directory`, then run
`scripts/frontier/prove-cleared-site.playwright.js` through the browser connection with the page as its argument. It
selects both site kinds and clicks the flag at both camera distances, checks the resource-reward and empty-credit
examples and model requests, saves four captures and closes each WebGL page. Strip the file's final semicolon when
passing its function as browser code. Keep the returned result beside the captures. Reuse this proof when revising the
marker.

Play's paths have a command: `pnpm --dir apps/game prove:play --base-url https://127.0.0.1:<port> --out <dir>` drives
Play against a running dev build. It opens each lab fixture and the live page signed out at desktop and phone sizes,
counts what is on the page, presses each row's verb and checks that it reached that row's own game or results
(`scripts/prove-play.mjs`, writing `play-proof.json` and screenshots into a new directory per run, and exiting 1 unless
every press arrived). The places beyond Play have their own:
`pnpm --dir apps/game prove:shell --base-url https://127.0.0.1:<port> --out <dir> [--state <file>]` presses each flow of
Season, What's new (from You, a section's tab chosen), the terms from the footer, You and Settings from a fresh Play at
both sizes, as a new visitor or, given a saved signed-in browser state, as a returning player, scrolling to and clicking
each control through the browser. You is the bar's place named You: on a desktop the player's name and portrait at the
bar's end (named "You: <name>" for assistive technology), through which a returning player reaches Settings and Sign
out; a Season board counts as shown with its first place or its line saying no one is on it yet. Signed out it also
measures the lab's fixture pages (Play, You, Settings, What's new the legal pages, each sign-in step, the doorway's
holds and an unknown path) and holds every page it passes, lab or live, to its content (words, controls, fields or a
card's picture) starting within 64 px under the bar (or a phone's top row), every word displaying at 14 px or more as
drawn (an SVG's scale counted), and on desktop the body starting at the window's left edge. It sets back what it changes
(the alert level, music) to the value it read, however the flow ends, and stops before anything outward (paying, leaving
for the docs, signing out). It writes `shell-proof-<persona>.json` and screenshots into a new directory per run, marks a
flow stopped by a service that did not answer as unavailable and any other stop as failed, and exits 1 unless every flow
arrived (`scripts/prove-shell.mjs`; the proofs share `scripts/prove-run.mjs`). Given `--watch /g/<chain>/<game>` and the
saved state of a player who holds realms in that live game, it drives the one path inside a match it covers instead: it
watches the game, opens the identity chip, presses its Play, and checks at both sizes that the doorway shows and goes,
the game is played rather than watched, the chip names the player, and the page never reloads
(`shell-proof-watch-to-play.json`). `apps/game/AGENTS.md` documents a spectator smoke test (agent-browser steps that
open the client, watch a running game and check the canvas). Inside a match,
`prove:play --game <chain>:<game> --profile <dir>` drives one game signed in (a browser profile signed in once through
the game's own sign-in, kept outside the repo): `--until-end <minutes>` stays on the map until the game ends and leaves
through **Game finished** → **See the results**, checking the page logged no "No game client is active"; `--ended`
presses **Results** on the ended game's doorway and on its map address, at desktop and phone; `--at-rest --watch` opens
the map and realm as a spectator, so a live game the profile holds no seat in shows its HUD at both sizes
(`scripts/prove-play-game.mjs`, writing `game-proof.json` and screenshots into a new directory per run). The outpost
proof below drives the runtime lab; live actions still need an eligible player.

Inside a Frontier match, `pnpm --dir apps/game playtest [--player <email>]` plays it as a spectator or a signed-in
player and judges each step by what the page shows (`apps/game/AGENTS.md`); a player signs in through the bar's Connect
(`data-action="connect"`) and enters by its game's card's verb (`data-play-verb`, on the `data-play-item` naming the
game), whatever their words or places.

## Shell (outside a game)

On a desktop, every page stands on its painting (the age or mode it shows, else the app's landscape) under the app
sheet's bar: the brand's lettered mark (56 px; 48 px in a phone's top row), Play, Season and Chat with their icons, the
page's notice, and the player (name and portrait, or You) at its right end. `Esc` goes back and `Enter` takes the page's
step, neither key printed on its button (`shell/frame/page-frame.tsx`, `shell/frame/tabs.tsx`,
`shell/frame/desktop-keys.ts`, the sheet's `AppTopBar` and `AppPageHead` in `ui/design-system/kit/app-elements.tsx`). A
full-screen step (sign-in) keeps the bar with the mark alone. The brand is the Realms Brand Kit v3: its palette
(`src/brand/palette.json`, read by `src/tokens.ts`), its faces (Realms Titling for one game's start on its card, IM Fell
English SC for headings, Cinzel for buttons, tabs and the bar's places, Exo 2 for everything read, the HUD included) and
its marks by displayed size (`REALMS_MARK` in `ui/design-system/icon-meaning.ts`: the lettered mark on the first frame,
the bar, the share card and the 512 px install icon, the small globe on the favicon (its master resampled, its ground
clear), a phone's home-screen and launcher icons and the status page, the contour globe on a loader and an iPad's
home-screen icons; `AGE_LOCKUP`, each age's complete lockup naming it on Play's cards and columns, Eternum's on the
kit's parchment grade), exported from the kit by `scripts/build-brand.sh <kit folder>`; the surfaces that cannot import
the token file (the boot, 404 and offline pages, the status page, the docs site's styles and config, the player emails)
hold a copy written by `pnpm --dir apps/game brand:surfaces` (`src/brand/surfaces.ts`). A link to the game or the docs
unfurls with one image, the lettered mark on the first visit's painting (`LINK_PREVIEW` in
`ui/design-system/icon-meaning.ts`, named by its content's hash), each environment's pages naming their own app's copy
(`play.dev-realms.party` on dev, `play.realms.party` on production; the docs' build reads the client's
`VITE_PUBLIC_ENVIRONMENT`). `src/brand/brand.test.ts` fails on a surface left on a v2 face, mark or colour, on a copy
that differs from what the token file writes, on a colour, face or mark a standalone surface writes on its own, on a
link preview not named by its content's hash, named by a surface of its own or served twice, and on email headers drawn
from older brand inputs (`pnpm --dir packages/notifications build:email-art`). Every page's content starts at the top,
under the bar; a painting is its ground, never space above it. `Enter` presses the page's step button (the one marked as
its step) when it is enabled, nothing has focus that answers Enter itself, and no dialog is open. On a phone the four
places (Play, Season, Chat, You; owner: "yeah B is great.") are a tab bar at the foot, 32 px icons over their words. The
app's notice (offline, an update ready, or Install, which only Play offers) sits in the desktop's top bar before You,
and on a phone just above the foot, one row: Install is its icon, Later and Install; under 1280 wide a notice with
action buttons shows its icon and verbs; a words-only notice keeps its text.

No page has Back and no row carries an arrow (the owner's pick A, 2 November 2026). Every page keeps the places, and the
place it belongs to stays lit as its way back; a phone's top row shows a place's lockup or another page's title. A row
opens on a press anywhere along it. A short edit happens in its row (Settings' name); anything longer opens in the app's
one sheet (`ShellWindow`: centred with its cross on a desktop, from the foot on a phone) or, in a game, the HUD's sheet,
each closed by its cross, a tap outside, a swipe down or `Escape`. A right-click submenu opens beside its menu.
`lint:guards` fails on a new Back link, back arrow or row chevron.

### Action windows

Full workspaces open in the centre of the desktop; on a phone they use the shared sheet placement. A headed window can
be dragged while open. Closing and reopening it, or replacing it with a different window, starts at its normal placement
again. Re-aiming the same open window at another tile preserves its current drag. Positions are not saved between
openings (`src/ui/design-system/molecules/popover.tsx`).

### Connected match proofs

Use a running client and a private browser profile signed in through the normal sign-in flow:

```sh
pnpm --dir apps/game prove:play --base-url <url> --game <chain>:<game> --profile <private-profile> --tasks boot --out <fresh-dir>
```

The **boot** task enters the game and opens Chat at desktop and phone sizes. The **chat-place** task selects the
player's realm, opens Chat, shares the realm with the place button, sends it to Global in words only that run writes,
drags the map away, and presses the place in that message: the map must select that very place (by its id; a site by its
exact tile and layer), centred on its tile, and on a phone show the map again. It requires the rendered map and Chat,
and fails on page or console errors. It is a signed-in game proof, separate from the signed-out Play/shell fixtures. The
same runner's
`blitz-build-window,blitz-farm,blitz-army,blitz-deploy,blitz-explore,blitz-transfer,blitz-transfer-window,blitz-transfer-gesture,blitz-standings,blitz-standings-detail`
tasks play a seated Blitz player's orders through the HUD (`blitz-standings` also checks the players' window fits its
table and rows, and `blitz-standings-detail`, at 1920x1080, that a player's detail opens beside the table without
stretching it); `blitz-attack-forecast` (Blitz) and `attack-forecast` (Frontier) open an army's attack forecast on a
tile it can attack, sending nothing, and check its verb stands alone at the right and the rest at the left; and
`gate-build,gate-production` check that nothing acts before play opens (`scripts/game-tasks.mjs`).

For a seated Blitz player with at least two armies able to act:

```sh
pnpm --dir apps/game playtest --base-url <url> --player <email> --chain-id <chain> --game-id <game> --steps blitz-burst --out <fresh-dir>
```

The **blitz-burst** step list cycles armies with Tab and orders reachable hexes by right-click, back to back. Its
verdicts match each click to the send's answer, Herald status and rendered army movement. It uses the shared playtest
runner, which retains transcripts and screenshots and closes what it starts;
[client instructions](AGENTS.md#playtest-the-one-command-that-proves-client-work) describe sign-in, renderer selection,
timing bounds and evidence. The proofs need a live game and eligible player; they do not create accounts or make a
signed-out fixture count as a played match.

## Frontier in a match

On a desktop, Frontier draws the approved atomized frame on one 16 px grid
(`src/ui/features/frontier/hud/frontier-desktop.tsx`, scaled by `frontier/hud/hud-scale.ts`: 0.93 at 1280, its own size
from 1440 to 1920, 1.05 at 2560). Top centre, on one axis: the place dropdown (`[data-place-pill]`, the same `PlaceMenu`
as a phone: **Realm / Surface / Veil / Hollow / Deep**, a locked depth with its lock), the day medallion (the bronze
bezel, ember in the day's last hour, the live arc and the day's number, the time left on its plaque; pressing it opens
**Today**), then the day's quest chip (its popover). Top right, the toolbar (`nav[aria-label="Places"]`, one order array
in `frontier-desktop.tsx`): **Research**, **Feed**, **Season**, **Settings**, each named, a press opening its page and a
second press closing it (Feed with its unread important count). The left column (`aside[aria-label="Realm"]`) holds the
realm card (its stores as round tokens and a troop strip, the next rank as a gold button only when it can be taken, else
a quiet label; no building list, a building's card is its one place), **Armies** under it (every open slot and army in
hairline rows, then one line naming the next locked slot), and on the World view the minimap at its foot
(`section[aria-label="Minimap"]`). The right rail (`[data-frontier-rail]`, 384 px) starts its panels at 72 px: **Feed**
opens in `[data-right-page]`, **Research** and **Season** (when `RaceRules` stands) take the wide stage from the left
column to the right edge (`[data-wide-page]`), and every action sheet (**Build**, **Castle**, building card, **Deploy**,
**Army**, **Today**, **Shop**, **Settings**, map site cards) docks in `[data-rail-panel]`. **Chat** stands in its dock
at the bottom right (`.frontier-chat-dock`, the rail's width): a strip with the last message and the unread count
(`button[aria-label="Chat strip"]`) that opens a 340 px pane above it; Escape or a click on the map closes it. The
**Feed** (`frontier/hud/frontier-feed.tsx`) is the world's history live, one line a story (its act's icon, its time, who
did what to what, how it went, what it paid at its end), your own in gold; each row's painting is its story's act
(`storyMeaning`) and a site's guard as the map draws it, under **All** (first and the default), **Important**, or
**Mine**, the lenses in the Feed's head. A point spent reads "spent a point on Battle IV"
(`/lab/game?scenario=point-spent`, **Feed**). The Feed tells only what the game writes as a story: a research purchase
has none, so it has no row until the contracts write one. Whole-screen proofs for the Season race and in-game panels
(Research, Feed, Chat, Settings) run as `lab-race` (`/lab/game?scenario=race`) and `lab-panels`
(`/lab/game?scenario=panels`). A desktop has no Menu: **Settings** in the toolbar holds Alerts, Leave game and How to
play. Frontier has no Next up (owner, 4 November 2026: "Do cut next up indeed for frontier"), so Settings draws no Next
up switch there. Leaving by a phone's Menu, Exit or by Back keeps the game booted: Back from the map goes to the page it
was entered from, and the home card's Resume returns to the map with its labels and plates as they were
(`/lab/game?scenario=from-home`, `playtest --steps lab-reenter`); Leave game loads the page afresh. Orders go from the
map as in Blitz: hover a tile for its order, right-click to give it. On a phone it keeps its own bands
(`frontier/hud/hud-bands.tsx`, `phone-top-bar.tsx`): at the top, the filling circular `Day` ring (`Day` / number) at the
left beside four icon + capacity-bar resource gauges (**Essence**, **Labor**, **Wheat**, **Knights**), the compact Realm
chip and **Surface / Deep** (World only) on the row below, and the compact daily quest chip under them on World; at the
bottom right, one thumb-zone action bar (`PhoneWorldActions`) with a single **Realm** / **World** switch button,
**Season**, and **Menu** (with a single dot on **Menu** when any place has something new and no numbers on the map), and
**Menu** opens the four non-Season places (**Research**, **Feed**, **Relics**, **Chat**) from `hud-places.ts` as tiles.
On the Realm view idle, the 3D castle and plots stay unobstructed until the player taps the Realm chip to toggle the
Realm panel open on demand; the bottom row keeps the armies dock alongside **World**, **Season**, and **Menu**. On the
World view, the bottom row pairs the armies dock with **Realm**, **Season**, and **Menu**; **Menu** reaches Research,
Feed, Chat, Relics, Shop, Today, Season, Settings, Guide and Exit. A page or a sheet stands over the foot, as tall as
what it holds and at most 45% of the screen, and short enough that the map keeps at least 40% of it between the strip
and the place bar (never under 12rem; past that it scrolls inside), under one row (its name and its way out; a drag down
on it closes a sheet), its verb in its foot; while one is open, the stores' row folds away. Chat is such a sheet too.
Any screen held sideways below the desktop's width (a phone, a Fold's inner screen, a tablet) takes the sideways HUD:
the places in a rail at the left, the rest in one column at the right, a sheet in that column. An upright tablet keeps
the phone's bands, its sheets, the Oath pick and the picked army in a rail at the right, the realm card and the lesson
dock in the column left of it, the lesson dock at the foot. The map's share per device:
`playtest --steps lab-hud-devices --viewport <w>x<h>`, failing a state under 40%. Tapping a build plot keeps the realm
camera still. The realm view zooms out until every plot of the castle’s reach fits the screen’s width, including an
Empire on an upright phone. Chat's tabs are its rooms (Global; a game's own only where its mode keeps one, none for now)
and **Messages**, the private conversations. One panel at a time, on both (`frontier/hud/one-panel.ts`): a page or sheet
the HUD opens, the card of what the map selected, or the deploy order; the one opened last stands alone and puts the
others away, so no panel opens over another. A detail opened from a page (a Research row's sheet, a realm on the
Season's board) stands in the page's place, and its cross returns to the page. Dev: HUD → In game

desktop and a phone side by side.

## Frontier day end
