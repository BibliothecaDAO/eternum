Blitz Play Flow

The player-perspective flow map, merged with the Command Deck brief, the Blitz user stories, the label spec and what the UI Lab has shown so far. Where the sources disagreed, the ruling and its evidence are written down so the next iteration argues with facts. v0.3 adds the scale rule: the deck must hold at ten holdings and at a hundred.

Tempo classes kept from v0.1, one change

The core constraint stands: sixty minutes, one-minute army ticks, no immunity. Every flow is budgeted by how long a player can afford to look away from the map.

Class Budget Rule
Reflex under 1 s, one click Executes on the scene with a hotkey. The ghost renders at click, solid at pre-confirm. Never a modal.
Glance 0 clicks Always visible on a chip, the card, or a label. At most six numbers on screen at any time.
Deliberate 2 to 10 s A drawer that opens upward from the command card. The map stays visible and clickable.
Commit 10 s and more Changed: hold-to-confirm on the button, 400 ms, no dialog. The one modal in the whole hour is settling, and it happens before the horn.

Surfaces re-coded: the side panels are gone

S Scene Everything spatial: terrain, units, ownership tint and rings, ranges, paths, ghosts, fog, fire on a raided realm.
S+ Scene label One label family. Badge for kind, chrome for owner, name and count. Hover adds one row. Never actions.
H Chips Three floating chips: identity and clock, holdings, attention and rank. Read at a glance, never edge to edge.

K Keys Every repeated action gets one, shown on its button.
M Modal Settle, once. Nothing else.
W Web app Lobby, value, results, review. The client is play-only.

Built for scale v0.3 · ten holdings and a hundred

A Blitz lord can end the hour with ten realms, ten camps, ten rifts and three hyperstructures. An Eternum lord can hold a hundred realms. A roster fails past ten, so the deck is designed by exception: counts at rest, a queue for what needs me, groups for what I care about now.

Scale rule: every surface must read the same at three holdings and at a hundred. A surface that grows a row per holding is wrong. Counts at rest, a queue for exceptions, groups for intent.
Lab gate: the capture's strongest lord holds 3 settlements and 9 armies, so a lab-only stress setting treats the N strongest lords' holdings as the followed lord's, labelled on screen as merged. Every scale claim is checked at 10, 30 and 100 holdings on real geometry.

Challenges to v0.1 each with its evidence and a ruling

C1
Two side panels plus a bottom ticker.
v0.1 §3 keeps a left command sidebar (structures, resources, chat) and a right context panel, with the ticker and minimap along the bottom.
Ruling: no side panels. Selection context lives on the deck, mine lives on the rail, chat is one line.Evidence: the owner rejected a four-sided overlay on the lab's first draft; the brief and every RTS this game courts put selection at the bottom; the lab measured the map at 45 percent of the frame with panels on two sides and 84 percent without.
C2
Three clocks.
v0.1 §A lists a game clock with day N of 10 and a phase name, a stamina tick cue, and an automation countdown.
Ruling: one clock with three marks. Time left is the number; the minute tick and the ten-minute cycle are ticks on its bar. No day names.Evidence: the client's "day" is six army ticks with names like Dawn and Dusk that change no decision; stamina refills every minute regardless; hyperstructure points land per ten-minute cycle. The brief's rule is one clock; the earlier HUD's three clocks were an audit finding.
C3
Commit class means a modal.
v0.1 routes upgrades and other irreversible actions through a modal.
Ruling: hold to confirm on the button. Settling is the only modal, and it precedes the horn.Evidence: no immunity and a sixty-minute clock make every dialog a cost; the brief's interaction rules already specify the 400 ms hold; the stories' "Never" list forbids confirmation dialogs for reversible actions and modals over the scene.
C4
Realms on F1 to F9.
This was the lab's choice, not v0.1's. v0.1 uses 1, 2, 3.
Ruling, amended in v0.3: digits are control groups. Ctrl and a digit binds the selection, the digit recalls it. Blitz pre-binds the three starting realms to 1 2 3, so v0.1's habit holds and the scheme survives a hundred realms. Armies on Tab and Shift-Tab.Evidence: F1 opens browser help, F3 opens find, F5 reloads, F11 goes fullscreen. A fixed index breaks past nine holdings; groups are how every RTS handles two hundred units.
C5
Mnemonic command keys.
v0.1 uses Q W E as explore, move, attack modes and B, P, L to open build, production and logistics.
Ruling: positional grid, meanings fixed per kind. Army: Q move, W attack, E explore, A transfer. Realm: Q build, W produce, E transfer, A train. The key is printed on the tile, so no mnemonic is needed and no letter collides with a browser shortcut.Evidence: the lab's grid already shows the key on every tile and says why a tile is blocked; Deliberate tasks open from the same tile as a drawer, so B, P and L would be aliases for what the grid already does.
C6
Space focuses the last event.
v0.1 §4. The brief had Space toggling map and realm view.
Ruling: v0.1 wins. Space jumps to the last feed event, the way an RTS jumps to the last alert. Entering a realm is a click on its tile or Enter with it selected.Evidence: thirty years of muscle memory; the stories' feed rows already jump the camera (B52), Space is the keyboard for that.
C7
Suggestion chips in the top bar.
v0.1 §2.2 promotes the first-minute checklist to a chip, and asks whether suggestions become the onboarding surface.
Ruling: suggestions live in the deck's context slot when nothing better is there, and fade after each is done once. Never a chip.Evidence: six numbers is the Glance budget and suggestions are copy, not numbers; the First-Timer stories put learning on the deck (B11, B12).
C8
Spectators get no ownership chrome.
v0.1 §6.
Ruling: spectators get the same labels and colours relative to the lord they follow, minus the buttons.Evidence: B47 and B56; one mount tree, one layout, no per-component spectator branches.
C9
Per-owner enemy colours as label fills.
This was the lab's first reading of issue #4876.
Ruling: the ring and tint under the model carry the owner colour. Labels are one quiet material with a thin owner stripe. Issue #4876 is satisfied without the carnival.Evidence: the lab capture at tactical zoom showed twelve saturated fills louder than the selection; the scene is the place for whose, the label for what and how much.
C10
Which six numbers.
v0.1 proposes points, rank, points per second, day and phase, armies ready, arrivals.
Ruling: time left · points with rank · gap to the next rank · armies ready · attention count (threats and arrivals together) · the selection's stamina. Points per second folds into the rank line as "+N per cycle held".Evidence: C2 removes day and phase; arrivals and threats both mean "look here" and share one jump key; the selection's stamina decides the next order.

Open questions from v0.1 answered where the evidence allows

1
New surface or a redesign of the left sidebar and cockpit?New. The deck, rail and chips are built in the UI Lab as one mount tree and replace the sidebar, the bottom-right panel and the four modal shells when they land.
2
Is there a right context panel?No. Selection context is the deck. Labels never carry actions.
3
Which six numbers?See C10.
4
Mobile now?Desktop first for play. Mobile is spectate and review this pass; the lab keeps a mobile layout so the deck never assumes a wide screen.
5
Who is the caster?In-app viewers first: follow a lord, same labels, no buttons. Free camera and HUD hiding are a later mode for streamers.
6
Suggestions as onboarding?Yes, in the deck's context slot, fading after first use. Bracket-based fading is a tuning question once the slot exists.

Still open for the owner

a
Does the ten-minute cycle deserve a visible mark on the clock, or is the minute tick enough?It changes whether held hyperstructures feel like income.
b
Realm view: a separate camera mode, or the same map zoomed to the realm's hexes?The brief assumed two views. Continuous zoom could make it one.
c
Feed on the right edge as text, or under the deck as a ticker?Text rows keep the deck clear and give Space a target; a ticker keeps the right edge empty.
